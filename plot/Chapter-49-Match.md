# Chapter 49: Match

The morning had no business in it worth the name, and Lucian let himself sit inside that fact without reaching for anything to fill it.

Selene had commandeered the breakfast table for a full accounting of Talon's fledged brood — three chicks now, apparently, though her count had shifted twice already and showed no sign of settling — while Riven argued, with more conviction than the subject strictly warranted, that a hawk raised this close to a dragon roost ought by rights to be fiercer than any ordinary hawk in the North. Thorne said nothing, content to let his brother's certainty run its course, and ate with the unhurried calm of someone who had long since stopped needing to win every argument at this table to enjoy sitting at it.

"You are not even listening," Selene informed Lucian, without any real reproach in it.

"I am listening. You are on your third chick."

"It is the *same* three chicks. I am only more sure of them now." She said this with the particular gravity of someone who considered the distinction obvious and slightly disappointing that it needed explaining. "Lyra would understand. She always understands when I am counting something correctly."

"Lyra is not here," Thorne said, mildly, "which is presumably why you are explaining it to Lucian instead."

It was, Lucian thought, watching the ordinary noise of the table close back over the small interruption, exactly the kind of morning the house had not been able to afford itself in longer than he cared to number precisely — no report waiting in the solar, no decision pressing to be made before the day's end, only the plain, undemanding company of people who wanted nothing from him beyond his attention to a hawk's chicks.

It did not last past the second bell.

He found his grandmother in the small sitting room she favored for conversations she did not want overheard by the wrong ears, and understood, from the particular careful arrangement of her posture, that whatever she intended to discuss with his father this morning had already been rehearsed more than once in private.

"You are here early," Orlena said, not quite a question.

"I was told you wished to speak with my father. I assumed, wrongly or not, that it concerned the house."

"Everything I concern myself with concerns the house, Lucian. That has never once required an apology from me, and I do not intend to begin offering one now." She folded her hands, and something in the flat, unhurried precision of her voice told him this was a conversation she had entered already knowing exactly where she meant it to end. "Lyra is two-and-twenty. Beautiful, well-bred, admired by every house that has ever had cause to notice her, and unmarried. I do not say this as a criticism of her. I say it because it is a fact this house has been permitted to ignore for considerably longer than any other house in Kaelor would have allowed itself, and permission of that kind does not last indefinitely."

Vesper, arriving a moment behind him, took the news with none of the surprise Lucian felt on his own behalf. "You have raised this with me twice already this year, Mother."

"I have raised it with you twice this year because it has been true twice this year, and will remain true regardless of how many times either of us finds it inconvenient to discuss." Orlena's gaze did not soften for either of them. "I am not proposing a match today. I am proposing that this house stop pretending the question does not exist, before some other house decides to answer it for us on terms considerably less favorable than the ones we could set ourselves."

Lucian said nothing for a long moment, turning the shape of it over the way he had learned to turn over anything that arrived wearing more reason than he wanted it to carry. "Does Lyra know you intend to raise this."

"Lyra will know precisely when it becomes useful for her to know it, and not one moment sooner." Something in Orlena's expression made it clear this was not cruelty so much as a lifetime's habit of controlling the order in which difficult things were permitted to land. "I have found, across more years than either of you have been alive to witness, that a thing said too early costs a family considerably more than the same thing said at the correct moment."

He left the sitting room with the particular unsettled weight he had not expected a conversation about his own sister to leave in him, and found, walking the corridor toward the family wing without entirely deciding to go there, that his feet had carried him first not to Lyra at all, but to the quieter rooms where Lady Mira kept her own hours.

She received him with the same composed, careful warmth she extended to everyone in this house who was not quite family and not quite a stranger either, and something in her face, when he told her plainly what Orlena intended, was not the alarm he had half expected to find there.

"You look as though you thought I would be frightened for her," Mira said, setting aside the needlework she had not truly been attending to since he entered.

"Should you not be."

"I am many things where my daughter is concerned, Lucian. Frightened is not chiefly among them." She held his gaze, steady and unhurried. "I have watched Lyra spend two-and-twenty years making herself smaller than her own gifts, out of a loyalty to this family I have never once asked her to carry so heavily. A good match, chosen carefully rather than imposed carelessly, is not the tragedy either of you seem prepared to treat it as. It could be the first time in her life someone hands her a position rather than she having to quietly build one for herself out of whatever this house did not need."

"She does not want it."

"She does not want it today, spoken of by Orlena, in the shape Orlena will inevitably give it." Something almost gentle crossed Mira's composed face, brief and quickly folded away. "I did not say I wished it done Orlena's way, Lucian. I said I do not share your certainty that the idea itself is the enemy here."

He found Lyra afterward in her own rooms, sorting through a chest of fabric with the unhurried, contented attention she gave to almost everything domestic, and understood, watching her before she noticed him in the doorway, that he did not yet know how to say what he had come to say without taking something from her that the morning, until this moment, had not yet cost her.

"You have the face you wear when something has happened that you have not decided how to feel about yet," Lyra said, without looking up. "Sit, or tell me, or both. You are hovering, and it is unlike you."

"Grandmother means to raise the question of your marriage. Formally, and soon."

Her hands stilled over the fabric, only for a moment, and when she looked up at him her composure had not broken so much as settled into something harder and more guarded beneath it. "I see."

"I do not want this decided around you as though you were a duchy to be granted."

"No." Something almost fond crossed her face despite the weight of the moment, aimed entirely at him. "You never have, which is one of the reasons I have always trusted you with the parts of myself I do not show the rest of this house." She set the fabric aside entirely, and for a long moment neither of them spoke, the ordinary comfortable quiet of her rooms sitting strangely alongside everything just said into it. "I am not naive, Lucian. I have known since before you were old enough to notice it that this conversation was always coming for me eventually, the same as your own conversation came for you before either of us had any say in the choosing. I simply did not expect it to arrive on an ordinary morning, over a hawk's chicks and nothing else worth remarking on."

"What do you want me to do."

"Nothing, yet." She reached out and took his hand, the way she had taken it his whole life, in exactly the gesture that had never once changed shape around whatever else either of them had become. "Just be exactly where you are sitting right now when the rest of it finally does arrive. That has always been enough from you before. I do not expect it to stop being enough now."
