# Chapter 61: Vow

Nearly a year had passed since the morning Lyra's letter arrived at Blackspire, and Lucian found, standing now at the rail of a Vaelmar-built ship with the wind off the open water pulling at his cloak, that he had stopped quite noticing the particular shape his own life had settled into across all those ordinary months — Dawn's recruitment steadily filling out past its first uncertain dozen names, the door of the Absence holding longer now than it had any right to after a single season's work, Selene taller by a hand's width and considerably less patient about being told so. None of it had felt, from inside any single week of it, like anything more than the plain accumulation of days. Seen all at once, from the deck of a ship carrying his whole family south toward a wedding, it plainly was.

"You have the face of a man doing arithmetic again," Selene informed him, wedged beside him at the rail with the fierce proprietary interest she had taken in every new sight since the coastline first came into view. "Riven says the water changes color the further south we go. I do not believe him."

"Riven says a great many things."

"He is occasionally right, which is the frustrating part." She squinted out at the water, unconvinced. "It does look different, actually. Do not tell him I said so."

Vaelmar's seat resolved out of the haze by midafternoon — Tidewatch, a sprawling fortress of pale stone built low and wide along a deep natural harbor rather than reaching for height the way Blackspire did, its walls given over as much to docks and shipyards as to any purely defensive purpose, a forest of masts crowding the water below it in numbers Lucian had never once seen gathered in one place. Riven leaned dangerously far over the rail to take in the whole of it, Thorne a steadying hand at his brother's collar without either of them remarking on the habit. Vesper stood near the prow beside Orlena, and Elara beside him, her hand finding his without either of them needing to look to know it was there. Sable kept close to the twins despite their being long past the age of needing it, and Dain stood apart at the rail's far end, dressed for once in something other than his usual severe black-and-silver, looking faintly, almost comically uncertain about what a Warden of Firsthold was meant to do with himself at a wedding.

Lyra was waiting on the docks when they came ashore, Mira beside her — she had stayed on at Tidewatch through the year's preparations rather than return north, close enough to her daughter's new life to help build it properly rather than simply hear of it secondhand — and whatever composure the last year had built into Lyra dissolved entirely the moment Selene broke from the family and ran the length of the pier to reach her.

"You got taller," Lyra said, crouching to catch her properly, laughing into her sister's hair. "That is deeply unfair of you, doing that without my permission."

"I wrote you about it. You did not believe the numbers."

"I believed the numbers. I simply did not believe how much taller they would feel, standing here." She held Selene at arm's length, studying her the way she studied everything that mattered, and then pulled her back in, tighter this time. "I missed you more than any letter managed to say properly."

Mira waited for the reunion to run its own course before she stepped forward, and whatever quiet composure a year of watching her daughter build this new life had settled into her gave way, for just a moment, into something closer to open feeling. She said nothing for a long moment, only held her, and when she finally spoke it was quiet enough that only Lyra was meant to hear it, though Lucian caught the shape of it standing near.

"Look at you," Mira said, quiet and unsteady in a way she rarely allowed herself to be. "I am so proud of you I do not entirely trust myself to say much more than that right now."

"That is more than enough." Lyra held her a moment longer, then drew back, something bright still sitting in her eyes. "You can say the rest of it later, when there is not an entire dock full of people waiting on us."

"I intend to." Mira's composure resettled, though the warmth beneath it did not fully leave her face. "Go. Be married. I will not keep you standing here a moment longer than courtesy already has."

The rest of the family closed the distance more slowly, and Lucian, reaching her last, found that she stepped into him the same way she had a year ago at Blackspire's own gate, entirely without needing either of them to arrange it.

"You look well," he said, holding her.

"I am well. Happier than I think I let myself believe I was allowed to be, most days, until I actually stopped and counted it up properly." She drew back, something bright and unguarded in her face that he had not seen on her in all the years before this one. "Come. Ellard has been rehearsing his welcome speech for a week. I would rather you hear it fresh than have him tell you so himself."

Ellard's welcome, when it came, carried real warmth beneath its formality, Meret at his side with the same steady grace she had shown at Blackspire, and Dunstan — heavier, plainer, standing a half-step back from his brother in the manner of a man more at ease overseeing a ledger than a ceremony — offered Lucian a firm, unshowy handshake and nothing more, which suited them both.

The wedding itself was held at dusk, on a wide stone terrace overlooking the harbor, Vaelmar's sea-blue banners catching the last low light alongside House Caldarion's black and silver for the first time either house's colors had ever hung side by side. Joren waited at the terrace's edge with none of his usual restlessness fully contained, his eyes finding Lyra the moment she appeared and not once, for the rest of the ceremony, finding anywhere else to settle.

The vows themselves were simple, the way Lucian understood most things worth keeping eventually became simple once the complicated work of arriving at them was finished. Lyra spoke hers plainly, without performance, the same honesty she had insisted on from the very first letter she sent home about him. Joren spoke his the way he spoke everything that mattered — a little unpolished, entirely unguarded, the restlessness that had first told Lucian everything he needed to know about him finally, visibly, given somewhere honest to settle.

"I did not come to Blackspire looking for a wife," Joren said, his voice carrying clearly across the hushed terrace. "I came looking for someone who wanted something as badly and openly as I have always wanted the sea, and did not expect to find her instead of it. I do not intend to spend the rest of my life wondering how I got fortunate enough that she wanted the same thing back."

Lyra's answer was quieter, meant more for him than for the gathered houses watching, though it carried anyway in the terrace's particular stillness. "I spent one-and-twenty years being careful on this house's behalf, and I did not expect the answer to all of it to be a man who simply asked me to be honestly, uncarefully myself instead. I do not intend to waste what that cost either of us to arrive at."

Selene cried during the exchange of the rings, unashamedly and without any of her usual bravado about it, and Lucian, standing beside her with a hand resting lightly on her shoulder, found that he did not entirely trust his own composure either.

Afterward, with the formal part of the evening dissolving into the easier warmth of a real celebration — Vaelmar's own musicians filling the terrace, Riven attempting and largely failing to master some local dance under Meret's patient instruction, Vesper and Ellard deep in a conversation that had plainly moved past politics into something closer to real regard — Lucian found Dain still standing near the terrace's edge, a cup in hand he had plainly not touched, watching the dancing with the wary attentiveness of a man assessing a threat he could not quite name.

"You look like a man waiting for the room to attack him," Lucian said, joining him.

"I have commanded field engagements with less uncertainty in me than I currently feel." Dain's gaze did not leave the dancers. "I do not know the steps. I do not know the songs. I am fairly certain Lady Meret intends to ask me to attempt both before the night is finished, and I have not yet decided whether refusing her would be more or less dangerous than trying."

"You could simply ask Sable. She seems to have found her own footing in it already."

"I noticed. I am choosing, for the moment, to be a coward about it instead." Something almost fond crossed his usually severe face, watching the twins across the terrace. "It is a good evening regardless of my own comfort in it. I do not begrudge it that."

Some while later, Lucian found himself standing apart at the terrace's edge, looking out over a harbor lit gold and orange by lanterns strung along a hundred masts.

"You are doing the quiet thing," Selene said, arriving at his elbow the way she always did when she judged him to have wandered too far into his own thoughts.

"I am allowed to, tonight. Nothing is asking anything further of me."

"That sounds like the arithmetic face again."

"Perhaps a gentler version of it." He looked out at the water, the whole strange, wide unfamiliar shape of this house that was now, in some part, his own family's too. "I was thinking that a year ago I would not have believed we would ever stand in a place like this, watching her this happy, surrounded by a house we once only understood well enough to be wary of."

"And now?"

"Now I am simply glad it happened the way it did." He found, saying it, that he meant it entirely — no reservation left in it, no careful weighing, only the plain, uncomplicated relief of watching something turn out well after cautioning himself for so long against expecting it to. "Come. I believe Riven requires rescuing from whatever Lady Meret is currently teaching him."

Selene grinned, already moving, and Lucian followed her back into the light and noise of the celebration, the whole of his family gathered in one place for the first time in longer than he could easily remember, and found, for the length of that one bright evening at least, that he wanted nothing more from the world than exactly this.
