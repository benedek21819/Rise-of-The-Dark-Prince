# Chapter 63: Kindled

The Shadow Commander of Dawn was waiting at the edge of the sealed training ground when Lucian arrived, and came to a precise, unhurried stop before him.

"Reporting as summoned, my lord."

"Caz." Lucian's acknowledgment carried none of the report's formality, the plain, unadorned familiarity of a name used long enough that it required no comment from either of them. "I have reason to believe something is moving toward this Duchy that I cannot yet name. I want every eye and ear this division has turned outward, starting today — movement along the southern approaches, unusual traffic through Firsthold's markets, anything that does not explain itself plainly. Report through you, and through you to me. Speed over precision, if the two ever come into conflict."

"Understood." Cazius inclined his head, the small, precise gesture of a man accepting an order rather than debating it. "I will have watch rotations adjusted by evening and the first reports collated within the week."

"Good. That is all for today."

Cazius turned back toward the ranks without further ceremony, already folding himself into the practical business of command, and Lucian left the training ground with the particular restlessness that came from having done something necessary without yet knowing whether it would prove sufficient.

He did not return to the solar. He found himself instead climbing toward the high roosts and past them, along the cut stone path that led deeper into the mountain's ancient cave system, the air growing colder and stranger with every turn until the ordinary architecture of the keep fell away entirely and the mountain's own older bones took over. The landing platforms gave way to the natural cave itself, stone worn smooth by generations of claws and wings long before this house had ever raised a single wall around it, torches set at wide intervals doing little against the deep, settled dark beyond their own small circles of light. He had made this climb countless times since childhood, and it had never once stopped feeling, on some level beneath conscious thought, like coming home.

Ancalion met him before he reached the cave's mouth, the way he always did now that his own senses had grown sharp enough to know Lucian's tread apart from any other. He had grown again since Lucian had last taken the time to truly look at him rather than simply feel his presence through the bond — broader through the chest, the black scales catching the torchlight in slow, deep ripples, a size that no longer felt gradually arrived at so much as suddenly, plainly here.

*You are troubled,* the bond offered, wordless and immediate, the particular flavor of concern Ancalion carried when Lucian's own feelings ran ahead of anything he had decided to put into speech. It had always worked this way between them, for as long as Lucian could remember — Ancalion reading the shape of him before he had finished deciding what shape to show anyone else, a kind of honesty no amount of careful composure had ever once managed to fully close off.

"I am uncertain, which is not quite the same thing, though I understand why it feels similar from where you are standing." Lucian settled against the warm stone near Ancalion's flank, the old easy comfort of it settling into him despite everything else sitting close beneath his ribs. "Something is coming. I do not know its shape yet. I have already set thirty-five people to finding out what I cannot."

Ancalion's great head lowered, resting close enough that Lucian could feel the slow, steady heat of his breath, and for a long while neither of them needed anything further than that — the particular wordless companionship that had never once required explanation between them, not since Lucian was small enough to be lifted rather than simply leaned against. He could still remember, distantly, being carried on Ancalion's back before either of them was old enough to be trusted with real flight, both of them clumsy and unafraid in exactly the way only childhood permitted. He could remember, too, the year Ancalion's growth had first outpaced his own, the strange vertigo of realizing the creature who had once fit curled against his side now had to fold himself carefully to fit inside this same cave at all. Whatever else had changed in the years since — the training, the recruits, the sword now sitting easy at his hip, the whole slow architecture of becoming this house had built into him one patient season at a time — this had not. Ancalion had never once needed him to be anything other than exactly what he actually was in any given hour, and Lucian understood, sitting here now with the weight of an unnamed threat settling into him, how rare and how necessary that particular kind of company had become.

It was only when Ancalion's attention shifted, briefly, toward the deeper dark at the back of the cave that Lucian noticed it at all — a stillness in him that read differently than ordinary rest, his great head turning by careful degrees toward a passage Lucian had never once seen anyone use. No one barred the way; the cave simply ran on far past the point any ordinary purpose required, deeper into the mountain than a man could walk and return from in a single day, and so it had simply gone unexplored for longer than anyone still living could say with certainty why. He listened, following Ancalion's attention, and caught, or thought he caught, something at the very edge of hearing — not a sound exactly, more the memory of one, a low resonance felt through the stone rather than heard through the air, gone again before he could be certain it had been there at all.

"Is something back there?"

Ancalion's answer came through the bond rather than any outward sign, and it was not quite an answer at all — something closer to a wariness that was not fear, an awareness held carefully apart from alarm, the particular deference of a creature acknowledging something older than himself without needing to understand it fully to respect it.

Lucian considered the dark passage a moment longer, curiosity moving through him in a way he did not entirely trust himself to indulge. He had grown up inside a house that measured trust in careful, deliberate stages — his father's own Domain unexplained to him until it mattered, Mercy withheld until he had proven himself worthy of carrying an ending, the Shadow Guard's own deepest workings still only partly his to command. He had learned, across all of it, that an unexplored corner of this house was rarely left unexplored by pure accident, and that pressing into one uninvited had never once earned him anything but a longer wait for the moment it would open to him properly. Whatever waited that far down, it had waited long before he was trusted with the rest of Blackspire's older secrets, and something in Ancalion's own careful stillness told him this was not an evening for walking further into a passage that had never once been offered to him. He let the thought go, the way he had learned to let a great many things go in a house built on secrets kept even from its own family until the moment they were judged necessary.

"Another time, perhaps," he said, more to himself than to Ancalion, and settled back against the warm scale beside him, letting the mountain's old stillness and the dragon's steady breath carry him further from the day's uncertainty than any solar conversation had managed.

He stayed until the torchlight burned low, saying little, needing to say little, Ancalion's steady presence beside him doing more to settle the day's unease than any single conversation had managed since the moment Cazius's report first ended and the real weight of the day began. The mountain held its own old quiet around them, patient and indifferent to whatever politics moved through the halls far above, and Lucian found, sitting inside that quiet, that some part of him had needed exactly this and had not known how to ask for it in any language except simply climbing the path and arriving.

He rose eventually, and Ancalion lifted his head to watch him go, the bond carrying something wordless and steady that needed no translation — not reassurance exactly, only the plain fact of presence, offered without condition and without expiration. Lucian rested a hand against the warm scale of his jaw a moment longer than the gesture strictly required.

"Whatever this turns out to be," he said, quiet enough that it was meant only for the two of them, "I do not intend to face it without you beside me. I never have."

Something in the bond answered that felt, as near as he could name it, like agreement already settled long before the words arrived.

He walked back down toward the ordinary noise of the keep, and found, descending, that whatever waited in the dark at the back of that cave, and whatever waited beyond the southern approaches his own division was now watching, he had at least this much steadier ground to stand on before either of them arrived.
