# Chapter 63: Kindled

Lucian found, crossing the Shadow Guard's own sealed training ground toward the assembled ranks, that he no longer noticed the transition from the ordinary corridors above into the Guard's own restricted ground the way he once had. It had simply become one more place in this house that answered to him.

The Shadow Commander of Dawn was already waiting near the ranks' edge when he arrived, and came to a precise, unhurried stop before him, offering the report with the exact, economical formality he gave every report, regardless of who stood to receive it.

"Shadow Commander, reporting as summoned. Thirty-one present and accounted for. Four more finishing a final assessment in the lower caverns that could not be moved without costing them the evaluation entirely."

"Caz." Lucian's acknowledgment was brief, the plain, unadorned familiarity of a name he had used long enough that it no longer required comment from either of them, though it changed nothing in how the rest of the exchange would proceed. "That is acceptable. I have work for the ranks today, and I would rather deliver it myself than have it filter down through three retellings."

"Understood." Cazius fell into step a half-pace behind him, precisely where rank placed him, and said nothing further until Lucian had reached the front of the assembled ranks.

Lucian's gaze moved over them — young faces, most of them, disciplined into a stillness that had cost real years to build, the same patient work he had watched grow from a windowless room and a stack of files into something with actual weight now. He did not intend to tell them what he did not yet know. He intended to give them something more useful than reassurance.

"You are not here today to be told a threat is coming," Lucian said, his voice carrying without needing to be raised. "You already know your work well enough to understand that threats are rarely announced before they matter. What I am telling you is this: from today, every eye and ear this division has is to be turned outward. I want movement along the southern approaches watched and reported, however small. I want any unusual traffic through Firsthold's lower markets flagged the same day it is seen, not the same week. I want anything moving toward this Duchy that does not explain itself plainly brought to Commander Cazius, and through him, to me."

He let that settle before he continued. "You are not soldiers waiting to be told when to fight. You are this house's eyes in places its soldiers cannot reach. I am asking you to be exactly that, starting now, and I expect the first reports within the week, not when it becomes convenient to compile them."

A ripple moved through the assembled ranks, controlled but real — not fear, Lucian thought, watching it, but the particular sharpening that came over people given a task with real teeth in it rather than another round of drilling against an enemy that never arrived. His gaze caught, briefly, on a face near the second row he recognized without needing to search for the name — the boy from Harrow's sworn lands, older now, steadier, the discretion that had once earned him a mark against his file long since settled into something Lucian no longer worried over watching closely. Beside him stood the boy who had hesitated at the final stage of a kill exercise well over a year past, the hesitation Lucian had argued was worth keeping rather than training out of him. Both of them stood straighter than most in the ranks around them, and Lucian found some quiet, private satisfaction in that, watching the two judgments he had made in a windowless room stand there now as grown men rather than files on a table.

"Questions on the assignment go to Commander Cazius," Lucian said. "He will bring me whatever he judges worth my direct attention. I do not expect any of you to make that judgment alone."

Cazius stepped forward at that, addressing the ranks directly for the first time. "Watch teams will be reassigned by pairs before evening. Those already posted along the Ascend's lower checkpoints will hold their positions and simply widen their attention outward. Those newly assigned to Firsthold's markets will report to the steward's own household for cover placement, not to the garrison — I do not want thirty-five faces suddenly recognizable to anyone paying attention to who wears Caldarion colors this month. Discretion is not a suggestion in this assignment. It is the entire point of it."

"And if something is found that will not wait for the weekly collation," a voice near the back asked.

"Then it does not wait," Cazius said, without hesitation. "You bring it to me directly, at any hour, and I decide whether it reaches the young lord the same day or the same hour. That judgment is mine to make so that thirty-five of you are not each separately deciding how urgent your own information is. Is that understood."

A murmur of assent moved through the ranks, and Cazius returned his attention to Lucian with the same precise formality he had opened with.

He stepped back, and Cazius moved forward to take the ranks through the practical shape of what came next — reporting channels, watch rotations along the southern routes, the particular discipline of gathering information without ever once being seen doing it. Lucian watched from the edge of the ground, satisfied with the precision of it, the same exacting standard he had come to expect from every task Cazius was handed.

Cazius rejoined him once the ranks had dispersed to their new assignments, his bearing still carrying the same controlled formality it had held since the report first began.

"You did not tell them what they are watching for," Cazius said. It was not quite a question.

"I do not yet know precisely what they are watching for. I know it involves the South, and I know the warning that told me so was not sent carelessly." Lucian held his gaze, the same level weight he gave any order he expected to be carried out without needing it explained twice. "I would rather thirty-five sets of eyes gathering everything than a smaller number waiting for a target I cannot yet name for them."

"That is sound reasoning." Cazius inclined his head, the small, precise gesture of a subordinate accepting an order rather than a friend agreeing with it. "I will have the first watch rotations in place by tomorrow, and reports collated within the week, as instructed."

"One further matter." Lucian kept his voice level, the particular tone he used when he wanted a thing understood as an order rather than a request. "If anything crosses the southern approaches that looks like more than ordinary traffic — armed movement, unusual numbers, anything resembling a force rather than a patrol — that comes to me immediately, day collation or not. I do not want precision purchased at the cost of speed, not on this."

"Understood. I will make certain every pair posted south knows the distinction before nightfall." Cazius held his gaze a moment longer, the same exacting attention he gave any instruction he intended to execute without error. "Is there anything further, my lord."

"No. That is all for today."

"Then I will see it done." Cazius turned back toward the dispersing ranks without further ceremony, already folding himself back into the practical business of command.

Lucian looked once more at the training ground, the ranks already settling into their new duties under Cazius's direction, and found, walking out into the colder corridor beyond it, that he trusted the division's discipline considerably more than he trusted his own understanding of what it was about to be asked to find.

He found his father in the solar not long after, and gave the account of it plainly — the assignment, the reporting structure, the standing order that speed take precedence over precision if the two ever came into conflict.

Vesper listened without interrupting, and considered it a moment once Lucian had finished. "You did not wait for me to authorize this."

"You told me, weeks ago, that whatever this turns out to be, I would not have the luxury of waiting for certainty before acting on it. I judged that this qualified." Lucian met his father's gaze without hedging the decision. "I did not clear it with you first because I did not believe I needed to. I am telling you now because you should hear it from me rather than discover it secondhand."

"That is precisely the correct order to have done both of those things in." Something settled in Vesper's face, quiet approval rather than open praise. "Thirty-five sets of eyes turned outward is not nothing, whatever this turns out to be. You have done well."

"It is not enough yet. Not against something we cannot name."

"No. It rarely is, at the start of anything worth doing properly." Vesper rose, crossing to the window with the particular unhurried habit he fell into whenever a decision had settled somewhere past the point of further discussion. "But it is considerably more than nothing, and considerably more than we had this morning. That is how most real readiness actually begins, Lucian — not with certainty, but with the discipline to act honestly on the little we already have."
