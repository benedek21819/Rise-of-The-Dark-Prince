# Chapter 72: Instinct

Lucian was coming out of the sixth pass, climbing, with the wind tearing at him and the cliff face going by on his left, when Lirath stopped flying.

He saw all of it and understood none of it.

There was no blow. Nothing struck her. Korrath was forty feet off her flank and turning the other way and had not touched her, and she came out of a beat with her wings open and simply did not take the next one — went over onto her side in the air with her neck still working and her eyes still open, and slid off sideways down the face of the mountain like something poured.

*What —*

That was all he had. There was no thought behind it, no place to put it. He had grown up around dragons. He had watched Lirath fly since before he could walk, in weather that would have killed men, and there was nothing in any of it that made sense of an animal simply ceasing to hold the air.

She went down past the eastern shoulder and out of sight.

---

He had four seconds, maybe five, of nothing at all — the specific white uselessness of a mind that has been handed something with no shelf to set it on — and then Korrath came around and started for the bridge, and everything else in Lucian's head went out like a lamp.

He knew that turn. He had been watching Korrath turn for the better part of an hour and had learned him the way you learn a fighter's habits, and this one was not aimed at him and was not aimed at the roosts.

Two hundred feet below the fight, at the head of the last stone bridge where the rock ran out over the drop, his father was standing exactly where he had been standing since Lucian arrived. Not moving. Arms at his sides. In the open, on bare rock, with nothing over him and nothing around him, doing the one thing he could do and the only thing keeping any of them alive.

Roderic had worked out where it came from.

Lucian did not have to think about it either. He put Ancalion over on a wingtip and went down across the front of that turn and broke it apart, close enough that the wind of Korrath's head coming round took the breath out of him, and black fire went across the whole side of that face at a range that would have ended a man.

Korrath went off his line, and came back onto it, and Lucian got in front of him again.

---

What followed was the worst hour of Lucian Caldarion's life, and he would remember almost none of it afterward in any order.

He was not fighting Korrath. That word does not describe what he was doing. Fighting implies some possibility of winning, and there had never been one — the animal outweighed him by a margin that made every exchange a question of whether Ancalion could get out of reach in time, and the answer had been getting slower for twenty minutes.

He was *in the way.* That was the whole of it. Every time Korrath turned for the bridge, something black had to be across the front of that turn, and there was nothing else on this mountain that could be.

So he did it again. And again.

Come in from above and off the shoulder, because it was the only line Korrath could not answer quickly under the Weight. Flame across the eyes and the open mouth and the thin skin behind the jaw. Break away high. Turn. Come back from somewhere else and do it again before the turn could finish.

It was doing damage. That was the maddening part. Ancalion's fire does not char what it touches — it takes the color out and leaves it grey and dead-looking, and by the third quarter of that hour Korrath's whole left side was going grey in long ragged patches where the scale had stopped being scale. Anything else in the world would have been finished twice over.

There was simply too much of him. A young dragon has only so much fire in him in a morning, and Ancalion had been spending his since first light, and every pass bought less than the one before it.

The margins got thinner every time.

Ancalion was going. Lucian could feel it through the bond in a way that had nothing to do with sight — the wing torn twice now and tearing further with every hard bank, the deep burn starting in the chest, the specific grinding slowness of an animal that had been in the air since first light and had already spent two days doing this. He was still the fastest thing in that sky. He was not fast enough for much longer, and both of them knew it, and neither of them did anything about it.

Twice Korrath got a claw into them. The second time it went into the meat of Ancalion's shoulder and came out again and Lucian felt his own vision go grey at the edges with a pain that was not his.

He did not look at the bridge. He could not spare the half-second, and he did not need to, because he could see the answer in Korrath — as long as that animal was flying like a barge, as long as every turn cost him double, his father was alive down there and still holding.

The Weight was his father's pulse. That was how Lucian knew.

---

Somewhere in the middle of it he reached for the Absence.

He did not decide to. It came the way the flame came, without instruction — some part of him underneath the exhaustion reasoning that if Korrath could not fix on where he was, Korrath could not meet him coming in.

It opened.

Not the way it opened in a quiet room with Dain standing over him and his whole attention on it. It opened *wide*, and it stayed, and he held it across three passes and two hard turns while the world came apart around him and his dragon bled, and he did not have one single second free to notice that it should have been costing him something.

It shaved a half-beat off Korrath's answer. Perhaps less.

That half-beat was the only reason Ancalion came out of the fourth of those passes at all.

---

By the end he was not managing it any more.

He came around wide over the drop with nothing left to trade — Ancalion low and heavy in the air, dragging the torn wing, the beat gone ragged and shallow — and saw Korrath already inside the turn he should have blocked, already lined up, already going down the long slope of air toward the bridge with his neck out.

Lucian had one option and it was not a good one, and he took it, and Ancalion answered because Ancalion had never once in eighteen years not answered.

They went in on the shoulder from above and took the impact head-on.

It was like flying into a wall that was moving. Something in Ancalion's foreleg gave with a sound that came up through the harness. They came off Korrath tumbling, half-inverted, losing four hundred feet before the wings caught anything at all, and Lucian's whole world reduced to holding on to a black neck and the raw certainty that there was not going to be another one of those.

But Korrath's line was broken.

And the animal turned, at last, away from the bridge and toward the two of them, because they had finally made themselves the more urgent problem — and Lucian, hanging in the air three hundred feet off the cliff face on a dragon that could barely hold altitude, understood with complete clarity that he had bought his father perhaps ninety seconds at the cost of everything he had left.

Korrath came.

---

The mountain opened.

Lucian felt it before he heard it, through the bond, and what came through the bond was not fear.

Ancalion — mid-beat, wounded, with several times his own weight closing on him and nothing but empty air behind — turned his head away from Korrath. Deliberately. Toward the black cliffs below the roosts, where a cave mouth Lucian had flown past a thousand times without ever once wondering about had gone from dark to something else.

Then it came out.

Lucian's first thought was that the mountain was falling — that a piece of the cliff had let go, because nothing alive comes out of rock at that size. It cleared the cave mouth and kept clearing it, and went on clearing it, and by the time the hind legs came free of the dark there was a shape over the eastern face of Blackspire that made everything else in that sky look like something from a different order of creature entirely.

Dark grey, with green in it where the light caught. Scarred everywhere — a body that had been written on. It came out slowly. That was the part Lucian could never afterward reconcile: the thing did not burst from the mountain, it *emerged*, unhurried, old, taking its time about the first few beats the way something very large and very old takes its time about standing up.

And then it was moving properly, and unhurried stopped being the word.

Korrath saw it.

Whatever else happened on that mountain, Lucian would keep this: he watched a dragon that had spent an hour hunting a man on a bridge, that had killed dragons in a war and enjoyed the work, come out of his turn and stop.

Korrath did not know what it was. There was no history in it — Lucian could see that even then, in the plain animal blankness of the way the head came round. He only knew what any creature knows in the half-second before something older and larger reaches it.

He knew he had made a mistake about the size of the world.

---

There was no fight.

Lucian had braced for one, out of some idea that this was how it went — that the enormous thing and the terrible thing would meet in the middle and it would be spectacular, and would take time.

It took about fifteen seconds.

Korrath went at him, because Korrath had nowhere left to go and was not built to run, and it was like watching a man swing at a landslide. The old dragon took the charge on his shoulder without appearing to notice it, got a foreleg over the back of Korrath's neck, and simply drove him down — down past the roosts, down along the black face of the mountain, down onto the broad rock shoulder below the platforms with a noise that Lucian felt in his teeth from three hundred feet up.

Korrath came down on his side on bare stone with his wings out wrong and one of them under him, and the enormous grey weight of the other pinning him at the shoulder, and he was still moving, still trying, still fighting to get his legs back under himself and his head around.

For half a second the neck came clear.

---

Lucian did not give an order.

He never afterward pretended otherwise, not to Dain and not to his father and not to himself. There was no order. There was no decision. He was three hundred feet up with his hands in the harness and his ears full of wind, and what happened next did not pass through him at all.

Ancalion folded and dropped like a thrown stone.

He hit the gap between the two of them at a speed nothing that size should survive, and there was no fire in it, because fire takes time and this did not have any. He took Korrath's throat in his jaws the way every predator that has ever lived takes a throat, low and behind the jaw where the scale runs thin, and set his feet against the rock and pulled.

It was over before Lucian had finished understanding that it had started.

The noise stopped. That was the thing he remembered — that Korrath had been making noise the entire time, all of it, the whole hour, and then he was not.

A man came off the far side in the tangle of it and went twenty feet down the rock and lay still.

Ancalion stood over what he had done with his sides heaving and his ruined wing hanging, and the great grey shape stepped off the body without any particular interest and looked at him for a moment, and then turned away.

---

Lucian slid down off the harness onto the rock because his legs would not do anything more considered than that, and stood there with one hand on Ancalion's flank, in the wind, in the cold, on a shoulder of his own mountain covered in blood that was mostly not his.

He waited for it to arrive.

He had learned, in the last two days, that it was going to be a while — that whatever was supposed to come after a thing like this took its time with him and did not always come at all. So he stood and waited for it honestly, as he had the night before, and gave it every chance.

And what came, when it came, was very simple and took no time at all.

*I would have told him to do that.*

Not *I am glad he did.* Not *it had to be done.* He turned it over the way he turned over everything now and found it exactly as it was: if he had been asked, in that half-second, with the neck clear — if there had been time to be asked, if anyone had thought to ask — he would have said do it, and he would not have needed the half-second, and there was no part of him anywhere, not one, that wanted it to have gone another way.

His dragon had not consulted him. His dragon had not needed to.

He put his forehead against the black scale of Ancalion's shoulder and stood there a while, and did not examine it any further than that.
