# Chapter 20: Trust

The request came on the third day of the visit, delivered the way Seren delivered most things — directly, and without much regard for whether the room around her was ready to receive it.

“I want to see him,” she said. “Your dragon.”

They were in one of the lesser sitting rooms, the adults occupied with the slow business of formal talk a few paces off, and she had simply turned from whatever she had been half-listening to and said it, plainly, as though it required no more justification than asking for water. Lucian supposed, to her, it did not. Every dragon in the realm had to be reported to the crown the moment it hatched or bonded. She had likely known his name and Ancalion's in the same breath for most of her life, the way she knew the names of the other Great Houses' dragons — Korrath, Nereus, Ashira — facts of the world rather than secrets kept from her.

“He is not tame,” Lucian said. It was not a refusal. It was accurate.

“Solara is tame,” Seren said, with the particular flatness of a complaint worn smooth by repetition. “She is my father's. I am allowed near her twice a year, when it is arranged, and never close enough to touch. You have your own, and no one arranges anything for you to see him. That is not fair, even if you are older than me.”

She said it as though the unfairness of that arrangement were self-evident, which, to a child who had never been permitted anything of her own that large, it likely was.

Vesper granted it before Lucian had to ask on her behalf, with the same brief economy he granted most things — a short exchange with the steward, a glance at Dain that settled the matter of who would oversee it, and the visit was arranged for the following morning with no more ceremony than that.

The climb to the high roosts was harder for her than it was for him. The air thinned quickly above the residential wings, and by the second turn of the stair her nurse had gone quiet in the particular way of someone rationing her objections for a moment that mattered more. Dain led without speaking. Lyra came a step behind Seren, near enough to catch her if the altitude or the cold turned her legs unsteady, though it never did. Seren climbed with her chin up and her breath showing pale and did not complain once, which Lucian noted the way he noted anything unexpected.

The platforms opened to the sky the way they always did, broad and ancient, the mountain falling away beyond the low walls in long white drops. The wind found them at once, sharper here than anywhere below.

Ancalion was already at the platform's edge, black scales without sheen, dark purple eyes fixed on the approach before any of them had fully cleared the passage. Lirath rested farther back near the cave mouth, a settled silver weight in the deeper shadow, her blue eyes tracking the new arrivals with mild, unbothered interest. She did not rise.

Ancalion did not settle so easily.

Seren stopped a full pace behind Lucian, closer to the edge of her own courage than she let show. For a moment she only looked — the size of him, the width of the folded wings, the way the black scales seemed to drink the pale morning light rather than reflect it. Her nurse's hand found her shoulder. Seren did not step back into it.

“He's bigger than I thought,” she said, quietly, for once without the flatness. “Solara is not this big, and Solara is already full-grown. She was already large before I was born.”

“He is still young,” Lucian said. “He will be larger yet.”

Behind them, Dain said nothing, but Lucian caught the small, considering stillness in his uncle's face that meant the observation had landed and would be turned over later, in a room without a princess in it. It was not the first time Lucian had heard some version of that comparison — Dain himself had said, more than once, that Ancalion's growth did not sit easily beside what was ordinary for a dragon his age. Lucian had not thought much of it before. He thought of it now, watching Seren's small, upturned face measure the black shape against whatever memory she carried of a dragon three times his age.

Ancalion's head lowered toward them, and something in the set of it changed as the distance closed — not toward Lucian, whose presence he had answered a thousand times without thought, but toward the smaller, unfamiliar shape half a step behind him. The great head angled. A low sound moved through his chest, not a warning exactly, but not the easy recognition he gave Lucian either. Dain's hand shifted, unhurried but ready, and Lyra drew half a step nearer to Seren without seeming to decide to.

Lucian felt it through the bond before he heard it in the air — a coiled, unsettled attention, sharper than restlessness, the particular wariness Ancalion carried for anything he had not already decided belonged.

“Stay where you are,” Lucian said to Seren, quietly, not urgent enough to frighten her further, exact enough that she obeyed at once.

He stepped forward instead, between the dragon and the girl, and set his hand flat against the black scales the way he always did, grounding the contact before he tried to send anything through it. The bond opened the way it always opened for him, wide and immediate, and this time he did not simply let it carry what it carried. He reached into it, deliberately, the way he reached into the Veil on the terrace — not force, only clear intention, held steady until it could not be mistaken for anything else.

*Mine. She is allowed.*

It was not a command. He did not have the shape for that yet, and some quieter part of him understood that forcing it would have been the wrong tool entirely, even if he had. It was closer to the trust Dain spoke of before a blade was ever drawn — offered plainly, and left to be believed or not.

Ancalion's head remained low, still fixed on Seren, and for a long moment nothing in him eased.

Then, faintly, Lucian felt it — not in Ancalion's posture, which did not change all at once, but in the texture of the wariness itself. It was not quite the same wariness he held for an ordinary stranger. There was a thinness to it, an uncertainty underneath the caution, as though the dragon were parsing the same unresolved shape Lucian himself had brushed against on the terrace days before — something that did not sit fully inside the boundary of *known* or *unknown*, *safe* or *not*. Ancalion did not have a word for it any more than Lucian did. He only held there, caught on it, the low sound in his chest neither settling nor rising further.

It was Lucian's own certainty, offered again and held without wavering, that finally moved him. The great head lowered another degree, the wariness draining out of the set of his shoulders by slow inches, until at last he exhaled — a long breath of warm, dark-scented air that stirred the loose ends of Seren's hair — and went still in the way that meant *decided*.

“You may come closer,” Lucian said. “Slowly. Let him see you do it.”

Seren came forward with less caution than the adults behind her would have liked, one hand already reaching before she was fully within range, and stopped only when Lucian's glance told her the last half-step was not yet hers to take. She stood there instead, close enough to feel the warmth radiating off scale and breath, and looked up at the whole dark length of him with an open, unguarded wonder that had nothing performed in it at all.

“He's beautiful,” she said.

Ancalion's eye, deep and purple and fixed on her, did not look away.

“You may touch him,” Lucian said. “Here. Not near the jaw.”

She set her hand where he showed her, small against the black scale, and went very quiet — the first true quiet Lucian had heard from her since her arrival. Behind them, Dain's hand eased down from where it had not quite left his side. Lyra let out a breath she had not announced holding. The nurse said nothing at all, which was its own kind of relief.

“He let me,” Seren said, not quite to anyone.

“He decided you were allowed,” Lucian said. “That is not the same as easy. He does not decide it for everyone.”

She looked over at him then, still with her hand against the warm black scale, and something in her plain, direct face rearranged itself slightly — not quite surprise, closer to the beginning of an idea she had not had a shape for until that moment.

“Does that mean you decided too?” she asked.

Lucian did not answer at once. He looked at her hand against his dragon, small and steady where it had every reason to shake, and found that he did not have a ready dismissal for the question the way he had for most things asked of him by strangers.

“I have not decided everything,” he said, which was true, and which was, for the moment, the whole of the answer he was willing to give.

Seren seemed to accept that as sufficient, the way she had accepted his first answer about Ancalion's cry weeks before — plainly, without pressing further than the room could bear. She kept her hand where it was a moment longer, and above them both, still and watching, Ancalion held the same unresolved, unnamed shape in the edge of his attention that Lucian had carried down from the terrace and had not yet found a word for either.
