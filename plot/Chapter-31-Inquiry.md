# Chapter 31: Inquiry

He told his father it was curiosity, and his father, who rarely accepted a reason without weighing it first, let this one pass without comment — which Lucian understood, by now, as its own kind of approval. If the heir to House Caldarion wished to spend an afternoon of the Aurelia visit at the kingdom's largest scholarly institution rather than in the noble quarter's idle drawing rooms, Vesper had no objection to raise, and perhaps, Lucian suspected, some quiet interest in what his son might come back knowing that he had not known before.

The truth was closer to Dorian than to curiosity. A morning spent circling a prince who understood his own house's weaknesses better than most of the court gave him credit for had left Lucian with the plain, uncomfortable knowledge that he knew House Dravenhall only as a name attached to a grievance, and House Kaelarion only as the family he was promised to marry into. He did not like operating from that little information. Dain had taught him that much before Dain had taught him anything about a blade.

The Hall of Inquiry did not announce itself the way the palace did, in light and marble meant to be seen from the river. It sat further back from the noble quarter's showier avenues, low and orderly, stone halls arranged with the same restrained discipline Lucian recognized, distantly, from Blackspire's own corridors — a building built for use rather than display, which made it, oddly, the first place in Aurelia that had felt entirely comfortable to walk into.

He gave his name at the entrance expecting delay, and got instead a young magister who introduced himself before Lucian had finished stating his business.

"Emeric Myrath," the man said, extending a hand with an ease that suggested he was either unbothered by rank or had simply decided the effort of performing deference wasn't worth his time. Ink stained two of his fingers, old enough that no amount of washing had fully lifted it. "House Myrath keeps a liaison here year-round, in case anyone from Aurelia's better families wanders in expecting an escort. I confess I did not expect the escort to be for Lord Caldarion specifically. We do not often see the North here."

"I am told the North does not often send anyone worth sending."

"That," Emeric said, with the particular brightness of a man who had just been handed an unexpectedly interesting afternoon, "is exactly the kind of thing I would like to correct, if you have the time to let me."

"I am told your Archive holds the fuller history of House Dravenhall," Lucian said, once the pleasantries had run their course. "And of the crown I am promised to. I find I know both houses less well than I would like, for a man who is expected to marry into one and answer for a war against the other."

"That is an honest reason to walk in our doors. Most visitors invent something grander." Emeric led him deeper into the Great Archive without hesitation, pulling records from shelves he clearly did not need to search for, and for the better part of an hour laid out for Lucian what the Hall's own histories held plainly, without guarding a word of it: the centuries Dravenhall had ruled Kaelor alone before submitting to Kaelarion's claim without a single battle fought over it, and the grievance that had somehow outlived every generation since for having no battle to blame; the shape of the war itself, decades past, that had settled Kaelarion firmly on the throne — Aldorion's unproven young claim, Roderic's father leading the coalition to break it, Kestrion's forges arming the rebellion, Thalorin and Vaelmar holding for Kaelarion while Myrath and Sylvaron kept their distance. "Your own house's part in it is not written here in any detail the Hall considers reliable," Emeric added, setting the last record aside. "Only that it came, and that it decided the outcome. The particulars were never given to us, and I have long suspected they were never meant to be."

"And Dravenhall's dragon. Korrath."

"Large and dangerous before the war, and just as dangerous after it. He came through the fighting essentially undiminished — which is, if you want my honest reading rather than the Archive's careful one, the actual reason Dravenhall still has a seat at any table worth sitting at. A humbled house with a dragon that size is not a house anyone finishes destroying, whatever they may have wanted to at the time." Emeric glanced at him, something assessing in it. "Lord Roderic inherited both the seat and the grievance from his father. He has given no one cause, in the years since, to say he inherited nothing else. Whether that is discipline or patience, the Hall does not presume to know."

It was more than Lucian had expected to be given so plainly, and he said as much.

"There is nothing here that endangers Myrath to share," Emeric said. "Dravenhall's grievance and Kaelarion's war are common knowledge dressed up as scholarship — every noble child on this continent learns some version of it before they are ten. I have simply given you the version with fewer omissions in it than the one your tutors likely used."

He led Lucian on toward the Map Room after, unhurried, filling the walk with the kind of easy commentary a good host offers a guest — the age of the eastern wing, a scriptorium fire two centuries back that had cost the Hall an entire section on Vaelmar's coastal charts, an old joke among the scholars about the Sea Watch's tower stairs. Lucian listened with half his attention and let the rest of it settle into the shape of the day, and did not think again of House Caldarion's own place in any of it until they had nearly reached the corridor's end, where a heavy iron-banded door sat apart from the ordinary run of reading rooms.

"Restricted Vaults," Emeric said, in the same unbothered tone he'd used for the scriptorium fire, when Lucian's gaze caught on it a moment longer than the rest of the corridor had held him. "Older records, mostly. Not much gets in there, and less gets out." He did not slow his pace to explain further, and the door passed behind them the way any closed door in an old building passes behind a visitor being shown the parts that are actually meant to be seen.

They parted at the Hall's outer steps as the afternoon light had begun to lean gold across the academic district's low roofs, and Lucian had already turned his thoughts toward the walk back when a small procession crossed the far end of the courtyard toward the Hall's private entrance — household guards in Kaelarion crimson, and at their center, unmistakable even at this distance, Queen Isolde.

She did not see him, or did not choose to. She walked with the same composed, unreadable grace Lucian had watched her carry through the reception three nights before, a woman who had spent two decades learning to let nothing show that she had not first decided should be seen. Beside her walked a man in Hall robes Lucian did not recognize, older and more senior than Emeric, speaking low enough that the exchange did not carry across the courtyard's width.

Lucian said nothing of it to Emeric, who had already turned back toward the Hall's doors and did not appear to have marked the arrival at all. He filed it away instead, the way he filed most things that did not yet have a shape — a queen visiting, quietly and without the retinue a formal audience would have required, the one institution in Aurelia that answered, in practice if not in name, to a house with no obvious stake in the crown's own family business.

It was not evidence of anything. He was careful, walking back toward the noble quarter in the lowering light, to remind himself of that plainly. But he had learned, across a year that had cost him more than he liked to count, that the things which were not yet evidence of anything were frequently the things worth remembering longest.
