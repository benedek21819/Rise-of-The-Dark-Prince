# Chapter 31: Inquiry

He told his father it was curiosity, and his father, who rarely accepted a reason without weighing it first, let this one pass without comment — which Lucian understood, by now, as its own kind of approval. If the heir to House Caldarion wished to spend an afternoon of the Aurelia visit at the kingdom's largest scholarly institution rather than in the noble quarter's idle drawing rooms, Vesper had no objection to raise, and perhaps, Lucian suspected, some quiet interest in what his son might come back knowing that he had not known before.

The truth was closer to Dorian than to curiosity. A morning spent circling a prince who understood his own house's weaknesses better than most of the court gave him credit for had left Lucian with the plain, uncomfortable knowledge that he knew House Dravenhall only as a name attached to a grievance, and House Kaelarion only as the family he was promised to marry into. He did not like operating from that little information. Dain had taught him that much before Dain had taught him anything about a blade.

The Hall of Inquiry did not announce itself the way the palace did, in light and marble meant to be seen from the river. It sat further back from the noble quarter's showier avenues, low and orderly, stone halls arranged with the same restrained discipline Lucian recognized, distantly, from Blackspire's own corridors — a building built for use rather than display, which made it, oddly, the first place in Aurelia that had felt entirely comfortable to walk into.

He gave his name at the entrance expecting delay, and got instead a young magister who introduced himself before Lucian had finished stating his business.

"Emeric Myrath," the man said, extending a hand with an ease that suggested he was either unbothered by rank or had simply decided the effort of performing deference wasn't worth his time. Ink stained two of his fingers, old enough that no amount of washing had fully lifted it. "House Myrath keeps a liaison here year-round, in case anyone from Aurelia's better families wanders in expecting an escort. I confess I did not expect the escort to be for Lord Caldarion specifically. We do not often see the North here."

"I am told the North does not often send anyone worth sending."

"That," Emeric said, with the particular brightness of a man who had just been handed an unexpectedly interesting afternoon, "is exactly the kind of thing I would like to correct, if you have the time to let me."

"I am told your Archive holds the fuller history of House Dravenhall," Lucian said, once the pleasantries had run their course. "And of the crown I am promised to. I find I know both houses less well than I would like, for a man who is expected to marry into one and answer for a war against the other."

"That is an honest reason to walk in our doors. Most visitors invent something grander." Emeric led him deeper into the Great Archive without hesitation, pulling records from shelves he clearly did not need to search for, and for the better part of an hour laid out for Lucian what the Hall's own histories held plainly, without guarding a word of it: the four centuries Dravenhall had ruled Kaelor alone, before the Year 620 grant folded it into an empire it had never sworn to; the peaceful submission that had ended the old rule without a single battle to point to, and the grievance that had somehow outlived every generation since for having no battle to blame; the shape of the Last Continental War itself — Aldorion's unproven young claim, Roderic's father leading the coalition to break it, Ironveil's forges arming the rebellion, Thalorin and Vaelmar holding for Kaelarion while Myrath and Sylvaron kept their distance. "Your own house's part in the war is not written here in any detail the Hall considers reliable," Emeric added, setting the last record aside. "Only that it came, and that it decided the outcome. The particulars were never given to us, and I have long suspected they were never meant to be."

"And Dravenhall's dragon. Korrath."

"Old before the war and older after it. One of the largest ever recorded on Kaelor, and essentially undiminished by the defeat — which is, if you want my honest reading rather than the Archive's careful one, the actual reason Dravenhall still has a seat at any table worth sitting at. A humbled house with a dragon that size is not a house anyone finishes destroying, whatever they may have wanted to at the time." Emeric glanced at him, something assessing in it. "Lord Roderic inherited both the seat and the grievance from his father. He has given no one cause, in the years since, to say he inherited nothing else. Whether that is discipline or patience, the Hall does not presume to know."

It was more than Lucian had expected to be given so plainly, and he said as much.

"There is nothing here that endangers Myrath to share," Emeric said. "Dravenhall's grievance and Kaelarion's war are common knowledge dressed up as scholarship — every noble child on this continent learns some version of it before they are ten. I have simply given you the version with fewer omissions in it than the one your tutors likely used." Something in his manner shifted, subtly, as he led Lucian on toward the Map Room after. "It is only when the questions turn toward certain other houses that the Hall becomes more careful about what it offers."

"You are careful with what you offer," Lucian said, somewhere past the second hour, when the pattern had become too consistent to be accidental.

"I have spent my whole career learning what is safe to know aloud and what is only safe to know quietly," Emeric said, and something behind the easy manner sharpened for a moment into a more genuine expression. "You would understand that better than most visitors we get here, I suspect, given your house's reputation for the same discipline."

"You know a great deal about my house's reputation for a man who has never met anyone from it."

"I know a great deal about a great many things I have not met." Emeric's smile didn't waver, but it thinned, just slightly, into something more careful. "It is the nature of the work. History does not introduce itself in person. One only ever meets it secondhand, in fragments, and spends a career trying to decide which fragments are telling the truth and which are only telling a story someone found convenient at the time."

"And which does House Caldarion's history tell?"

It was, Lucian recognized even as he asked it, closer to a real question than the idle shape of the afternoon had intended. Emeric's gaze held his a beat longer than the question strictly required, weighing something Lucian could not read.

"An incomplete one," Emeric said at last. "Which is true of every history old enough to matter, and no more suspicious in your house's case than in anyone else's. I would not read anything into that, Lord Caldarion, beyond what the words themselves say." He turned, gesturing toward a heavy door at the corridor's far end, iron-banded, plainly not part of the standard circuit. "The Restricted Vaults sit beyond there. I cannot take you in — that permission belongs to my aunt, and to very few besides her — but I mention it because you asked an honest question, and I did not want you to think I was hiding the existence of an answer, only the answer itself."

"Fair enough."

"Most people do not ask twice, once told that. You might be the exception, in time." Something flickered behind Emeric's eyes, curiosity folding briefly over itself, before he let the moment pass and turned them back toward the Archive's quieter halls. "For today, I think the Map Room has served you better than the Vaults would have. Knowing where your enemies stand is more useful than knowing where they came from. Usually."

They parted at the Hall's outer steps as the afternoon light had begun to lean gold across the academic district's low roofs, and Lucian was still turning the word *usually* over, weighing its particular deliberate weight, when a small procession crossed the far end of the courtyard toward the Hall's private entrance — household guards in Kaelarion crimson, and at their center, unmistakable even at this distance, Queen Isolde.

She did not see him, or did not choose to. She walked with the same composed, unreadable grace Lucian had watched her carry through the reception three nights before, a woman who had spent two decades learning to let nothing show that she had not first decided should be seen. Beside her walked a man in Hall robes Lucian did not recognize, older and more senior than Emeric, speaking low enough that the exchange did not carry across the courtyard's width.

Lucian said nothing of it to Emeric, who had already turned back toward the Hall's doors and did not appear to have marked the arrival at all. He filed it away instead, the way he filed most things that did not yet have a shape — a queen visiting, quietly and without the retinue a formal audience would have required, the one institution in Aurelia that answered, in practice if not in name, to a house with no obvious stake in the crown's own family business.

It was not evidence of anything. He was careful, walking back toward the noble quarter in the lowering light, to remind himself of that plainly. But he had learned, across a year that had cost him more than he liked to count, that the things which were not yet evidence of anything were frequently the things worth remembering longest.
