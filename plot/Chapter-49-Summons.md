# Chapter 49: Summons

A year settled over Blackspire the way years at Blackspire always settled — not as a single turning point but as a slow accumulation of ordinary mornings, each one indistinguishable from the last until enough of them had passed that Lucian looked back and found the shape of a boy had quietly become something else entirely.

He was sixteen now, though no one in this house had ever made a ceremony of the counting. The Absence had not become obedient in that time — he had not expected it to, not after Dain's own plain warning that no one could teach him the rest of it — but it no longer felt like a stranger reaching into him from outside himself either. He could hold Riven or Thorne's senses dulled for the better part of two full seconds now, under real pressure, sometimes three, and had learned to feel the moment it ended — his own vision and hearing, sharpened past anything ordinary while the Absence held, dropping back to their normal register all at once, and his hands unsteady for a moment afterward every time. Not control. Something closer to familiarity with an unruly guest who had, at least, stopped arriving entirely without warning.

The mole remained unnamed. Vesper's patient, narrowing work had, across the year, closed the circle of who could plausibly have known the Corvane parley's route down to a handful of names Lucian still was not permitted to hear — not out of distrust, his father had said plainly, but because a suspicion spoken aloud too early taught its target to go still, and they had already spent one season and a half learning exactly how well silence served a leak that knew it was being watched. The small, careful exchange with the Crown's own channel had continued in the same vein — a verified truth offered, a verified truth returned, neither side reaching for more than the last exchange had earned — without becoming the fuller alliance Isolde had once offered and without collapsing into nothing either. It simply continued, the way most of the truly important work in this house continued: unhurried, unannounced, and never once resolved cleanly enough to stop requiring attention.

The letter came on an ordinary morning in early autumn, carried into the solar by a steward who set it down with more care than the house's usual correspondence received, and Lucian understood, watching his father's face as Vesper broke the seal, that whatever it contained was not going to be an ordinary morning's business.

"The Convocation," Vesper said, after a long moment. "Every ten years, the Great Houses gather in Aurelia before the Crown. You have never seen one, because this house has never once attended in your lifetime, or in mine, or — as far as I have ever been told — in longer than that. An invitation arrives. It is read, and set aside, and no one at court has ever thought to press the matter, because Caldarion's absence from that hall has been a settled fact of the world for longer than anyone living can properly account for."

"And this one is different."

"This one is addressed to me." Vesper turned the letter so Lucian could see it — not the familiar, generic formality of "House Caldarion," the address every prior invitation had worn without ever once requiring a personal answer, but his father's own name, written plainly, in a hand too careful to be a scribe's habit alone. "In every Convocation held since I became head of this house, I have never once seen my own name on the outside of the envelope."

Lucian read it through himself, twice, and found what struck him most was not what the letter said but what it declined to. It named the Convocation's date, its location, the customary courtesies owed a Great House's attendance — trade, alliances, and the ordinary decade's business, unremarkable and unchanged from every prior cycle this house had simply declined to attend — and then, where a routine invitation would have ended, it added a single restrained line beyond the rest: that a separate matter, certain irregularities the Crown considered worth every house's attention, would also be addressed during the gathering, and that House Caldarion's presence for that portion of it, this time, was specifically and personally requested.

"It says almost nothing."

"It says everything that matters, if you know how to read the silence around it." Vesper set the letter down, and something crossed his face that Lucian had learned, across a year of watching him weigh difficult things, to recognize as genuine unease rather than the ordinary caution he wore for ordinary problems. "A house that wished merely to inform me would have listed its concerns plainly. A house that wished to be careful of what it committed to paper, in case that paper found its way to eyes it was never meant for, would write exactly this — enough to make refusal costly, not enough to hand any part of the true matter to whoever might intercept it first. I would recognize the shape of it anywhere. It is very close to my own doctrine, turned back toward me by someone who has clearly had cause to learn it just as carefully."

"You already know something of what this concerns."

"I know what every Great House has murmured about for the better part of two years now, the same as you do — strange things happening to the Veil in more places than usual, more often than usual, with no single cause anyone has yet named convincingly. I did not think it would ever reach far enough to require this house's presence at a table I have spent every year of my rule keeping empty." Something in Vesper's stillness deepened, private and unexplained, a door Lucian had learned not to knock on until his father chose to open it himself. "I cannot tell you precisely why the request is addressed to me and not merely to this house. I can tell you that I do not fully believe the reason is as simple as courtesy, and that not believing it troubles me more than I would like to admit to you plainly."

"Will we go?"

Vesper was quiet long enough that Lucian understood the answer had already been decided before the question was finished, and that the silence was only his father giving the decision its proper weight before he spoke it aloud.

"Yes. And you will come with me." He met Lucian's eyes, steady, the particular gravity he reserved for the decisions that mattered most settling fully into his voice. "Not as an afterthought to my own presence, the way an heir is sometimes brought merely to be seen. You will stand in that hall as House Caldarion's future in your own right, in front of seven other Great Houses that have spent a generation building an idea of this family out of absence and rumor alone. That is no small thing to hand you, and no small risk either, with our own walls not yet clean of whoever has been listening at them. I have weighed it. I would rather face that risk with you beside me than leave you here, unwatched, while I carry it alone."

"And Blackspire, while we are gone."

"Will be watched as carefully as it has been watched all year, by people neither of us will need to name aloud for you to trust that they are working." Vesper rose, and for a moment something almost like anticipation moved behind the caution in his face — the particular readiness of a man who had spent his entire time as head of this house avoiding a room and had finally run out of reasons to keep avoiding it. "We leave within the fortnight. There is a great deal to prepare, and I intend for House Caldarion's first appearance in a generation to answer every question it raises, rather than inviting a hundred more."

Lucian looked down at the letter once more, at his father's own name written where "House Caldarion" had always stood before, and found, reading it, that the unease in his chest was not entirely his father's borrowed caution. Something in the deliberate, careful silence of it felt, to him, like the particular quiet of a door being opened from the other side after a very long time shut — and he could not yet tell, holding it, whether what waited on the far side of that door meant to welcome this house back, or simply wanted, at long last, to see exactly what had been kept behind it for so long.
