# Chapter 58: Watch

A week had passed in the ordinary business of arrangements — terms exchanged with Vaelmar by courier, a detachment of Silver Guard selected and drilled to Vesper's own exacting satisfaction, Lady Mira's own preparations made with the quiet, thorough calm she brought to everything concerning her daughter — before the morning finally arrived that Lucian found himself standing once again at the great doors, watching Lyra's traveling party assemble in the grey early light.

"You do not have to come all this way to see us off," Lyra said, though the warmth in it gave away how little she meant the words as any real objection.

"I gave you my word I would be exactly where I am standing, whenever the rest of it arrived. I did not intend to make an exception for a mere departure." Lucian glanced past her to where Mira stood, composed and steady, already dressed for the road in a manner that told him she had spent the past week thinking of very little else. "You are certain of this."

"I am certain that my daughter should not travel a duchy away from her own house without her mother beside her, whatever else remains uncertain about where this ends." Mira's answering look held none of the wariness he half expected from her. "Lyra has spent one-and-twenty years being careful on this house's behalf. I intend to spend the next several weeks making sure someone is watching over her the way she has always watched over everyone else."

Vesper came to her before anyone else did, and whatever he said passed quietly enough that even Lucian, standing close, caught only the shape of it rather than the words themselves — something brief, something that made Lyra's composure waver for the first time all morning before she folded her arms around her father without any of her usual careful restraint. Vesper held her a moment longer than strict formality required, and let her go without comment, the particular economy of a man who had already said everything he intended to say and did not believe in saying it twice.

Orlena's farewell was shorter still, and no less felt for it. "You have chosen well, and chosen it yourself, which is the part of this I am proudest of." She touched Lyra's cheek once, brief and dry-eyed. "Write to me plainly, when there is something plain worth writing. I have never had much patience for letters that only flatter."

"I would not know how to write you any other kind, Grandmother."

"No," Orlena said, something almost fond beneath the flatness. "I do not suppose you would."

Selene, permitted this time to stand at the very front of the farewell rather than be tucked into some corner, gripped Lyra's hand with the particular fierce insistence she reserved for partings she had not fully made peace with. "You will write."

"Every week. Possibly more, if Vaelmar's coast turns out to be as interesting as everyone keeps promising me it will be." Lyra crouched to meet her properly, and whatever she said next passed too quietly for anyone else to hear, though it settled something visible in Selene's face all the same.

Lucian came to her last, and found, when Lyra turned to him, that neither of them had any particular need of words for this part of it. She stepped into him before he had fully raised his arms, and he held her the way he had held very few people in this house without some deliberate restraint governing the shape of it — simply, entirely, for as long as she needed it to last.

"I am not losing you," he said, quiet enough that it stayed only between them. "I need you to know I actually believe that, and am not merely saying it because it is the kind thing to say."

"I know you believe it. That is precisely why it helps." She drew back enough to look at him properly, some of her earlier composure returning, though not all of it. "Whatever happens south of here, you were the one who made certain I got to choose it honestly. I will not forget that, whatever else this year asks of either of us."

"Go," Lucian said, before either of them could make the moment last longer than it could comfortably hold. "Before Grandmother decides we have taken quite enough of everyone's morning."

The Silver Guard detachment formed up with the crisp, practiced discipline of men Vesper had personally reviewed for exactly this duty, and within the hour the party was moving down the long fortified turns of the Ascend, banners small and then smaller against the mountain's own vast scale, until the last of them finally passed from sight around the lower bend.

Lucian remained at the doors longer than the others, watching the empty turn of the road as though it might yet yield something further to look at, and found himself turning over, unbidden, the particular shape of the promise he had made her months ago in her own rooms — that he would stand behind whatever she chose, that nothing past those doors would change it. It had cost him nothing to say at the time. It cost him something now, watching her actually go, to feel how much weight a promise gained once the moment it was made for had finally, truly arrived.

He found his father still standing near the doors some time after the others had returned to the ordinary business of the day, and understood, from the particular stillness in Vesper's posture, that he was not the only one who had not yet fully let the moment go.

"There is something I should tell you," Lucian said, "before you hear it from anyone else and wonder why I did not say so myself."

"Go on."

"I have arranged for a Shadow Guard to travel unofficially alongside the party, at a discreet distance. Not part of the formal escort — someone who will not be seen unless he needs to be." Lucian held his father's gaze, steady despite the faint unease of admitting to an arrangement made entirely on his own authority. "I did not clear it with you first. I am telling you now because I would rather you know than discover it by accident."

Vesper was quiet long enough that Lucian began, briefly, to wonder whether he had overstepped some line he had not fully seen coming. "Who did you send."

"One of the men you gave me, to help build the new division's core." Lucian met his father's gaze without hedging the admission. "I trust him more than most of the formal escort, and I did not want Lyra's safety resting only on discipline chosen for the road rather than for exactly this kind of quiet, unglamorous watching. I judged the risk of overstepping smaller than the risk of doing nothing and later wishing I had."

"That is a sound judgment, and precisely the kind I would expect from you now rather than a year ago." Something shifted in Vesper's face — not displeasure, but a slow, dawning and faintly rueful recognition, the look of a man arriving at a realization he did not yet want to say aloud. "I sent men of my own as well," he said finally. "Two days ago. A pair from Dusk, with instructions to shadow the party unseen for the length of the journey and report back only if something required it."

The two of them stood with that a moment, the particular silence of two men realizing, at the same time, exactly how alike their instincts had turned out to be.

"You did not think to mention this to me either," Lucian said.

"I did not think there was anything to mention. I assumed the arrangement was mine alone to make." Something almost amused was working its way, slowly and against considerable resistance, into the corner of Vesper's mouth. "It appears we have sent Lyra to Vaelmar with a full Silver Guard escort, her own mother, and three entirely separate shadows, none of whom is aware the others exist."

"She would find that unbearably funny, if she ever learned of it."

"She will not learn of it, if any of the three is worth what we are paying him." Vesper's expression settled, though the faint trace of dry humor did not fully leave it. "I suppose it says something true about both of us, that neither one trusted the other's precautions enough to skip his own."

"It says we both love her enough to be foolish about it in exactly the same way."

"Yes," Vesper said, after a moment, something quieter than amusement settling into the word. "I suppose it does."

They stood together a while longer at the doors, saying nothing further that needed saying, watching the empty stretch of the Ascend as though it might yet give something more of her back before the day was through. It did not, and Lucian found, walking back into the ordinary business of the keep beside his father, that he did not need it to. Three shadows, a mother's steady presence, and a Silver Guard escort drilled to Vesper's own exacting standard were already more than most daughters of any Great House would ever be given. Whatever came of Vaelmar, whatever came of the months of watching and waiting still ahead of all of them, Lyra had gone south carrying more quiet love than she likely yet knew to look for.
