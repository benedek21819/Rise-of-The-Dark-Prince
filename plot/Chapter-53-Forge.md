# Chapter 53: Forge

"Send the invitation," Lyra said, three mornings after the letter, with the particular flat finality of someone who had turned a decision over long enough to stop needing anyone else's approval of it. "Not a summons. An invitation, worded as one, so that if I meet him and the answer is still no, no one can claim afterward that House Caldarion promised more than it did by asking him to come."

Vesper considered her over the rim of his cup, and whatever he found in her face settled the matter faster than Lucian had expected it to. "I will have it written today. Vaelmar will not mistake the wording, whatever Ellard's own hopes make of it privately."

"Let him hope. Hope is not a promise, and I intend to owe him nothing beyond an honest look at his son." She held her father's gaze a moment longer, some old, careful distance between them easing by a fraction now that she had asked for something plainly rather than waited to be told what the house had already decided for her. "Thank you for letting me be the one to ask."

"You should not have to thank me for that," Vesper said, and there was something almost rough beneath the words, an old debt he seemed to be paying down one small deference at a time. "It should simply have been so from the start."

The invitation went out that afternoon, and with it went the particular held breath the house had been keeping since the letter first arrived — not resolved, only given somewhere to go, a date and a courier and a span of weeks in which the question could rest without demanding an answer from anyone before it was ready to be given one. Lucian found, watching his sister return to the ordinary business of her days with something lighter in her step than she had carried in months, that he did not need to know what came of it yet to feel the weight of it lift a little from all of them.

His own days had a weight of a different, steadier kind, and it was to that weight he returned the following morning, in the windowless room off the lower solar that had become, without his quite noticing the transition, less a duty and more simply where he was.

"Tell me about him," Vesper said, sliding a thin file across the table between them — no ceremony to it, the same unhurried manner he brought to every candidate they reviewed together now.

Lucian had already read it twice. "Nineteen. Second son of a lesser family sworn to Harrow, no inheritance worth the name waiting for him there. Strong in the physical trials, faster than anyone else in his intake group over short distances. Weaker in the discretion trials — he was flagged twice for asking questions of his instructors that he had been told plainly were not his to ask."

"And your judgment."

"My judgment is that speed and strength are the easiest things to train into a man who already has the raw shape of them, and the hardest to train into one who does not. Discretion is the opposite. A man who cannot yet hold his own questions can be taught to, if the will beneath the curiosity is loyalty and not simple restlessness." Lucian set the file down. "I would not reject him for it. I would watch him more closely than the others for the next year, and be honest with myself the day I stop being able to tell the difference between his curiosity settling and his curiosity merely learning to hide better."

Something that was almost pride moved behind his father's eyes, controlled the way it always was, but present all the same. "That is very nearly the same judgment I reached last night, reading the same file alone." He set his own copy aside. "The difference between us is that I would have told you my answer first and let you agree with it, six months ago. You no longer need me to go first."

"I still value knowing you agree."

"That is not the same thing as needing it, and you know the difference better than you are willing to say plainly to my face." Vesper's mouth eased, briefly, into something not quite a smile. "Which is, incidentally, exactly the quality I am asking you to learn to recognize in every name in this room. A man who still needs to be told he is right is not yet ready to be trusted alone with the kind of work this division will eventually ask of him. A man who no longer needs it, but still wants it, generally is."

They worked through four more files before the light in the narrow window had shifted meaningfully, the particular unglamorous rhythm his father had promised him from the first day of this — no single name in a single sitting ever feeling like progress, the whole of it, taken together across the weeks now behind them, plainly becoming something. Lucian caught himself, setting aside the last file of the morning, thinking less about the man it described than about the shape of the year ahead of him: a hundred more mornings like this one, most of them exactly this quiet, most of them exactly this slow, and at the far end of all of it, a division that would answer to him and no one else.

"You are thinking about how long this will take," Vesper said, watching him with the particular attentiveness he gave to silences as much as words.

"I am thinking that I did not expect to find it satisfying. The slowness."

"Good." His father rose, gathering the files into their careful order before they were returned to wherever such things were kept when the room was not in use. "That satisfaction is the only thing that will carry you through the parts of this work that never once feel like anything worth being proud of. Hold onto it. You will need it long after today's four names have stopped mattering to you at all."
