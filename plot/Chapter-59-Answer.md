# Chapter 59: Answer

Two months of ordinary business had passed since the morning Lyra's party disappeared around the lower bend of the Ascend, and Lucian had settled, without quite deciding to, into the particular rhythm of a house waiting for something it had stopped mentioning aloud at every meal. The recruits moved steadily through their training, three more names crossed off the list entirely and two others quietly promoted past the point Lucian had once doubted they would reach. Word had come twice already from the operative he had sent south, brief and unremarkable — the party safe, the roads uneventful, nothing requiring more than the report itself.

The letter that finally mattered arrived on an otherwise unremarkable morning, carried into the solar the way every serious piece of correspondence in this house eventually was, and Vesper read the first lines of it in silence before looking up.

"It is from Lyra," he said. "I think it is better heard than summarized." He smoothed the pages against the table, and began.

*To everyone at Blackspire —*

*I have started this letter four times and torn up three of them, which should tell you something about how much easier it is to be honest with you in person than at this distance, where I cannot see any of your faces to know how the words are landing. I will simply write it plainly, then, the way I would have said it if I had come home first instead of writing ahead.*

*Vaelmar is not Blackspire. I do not mean that as either a complaint or a compliment, only as a fact I did not expect to find as disorienting as I did in my first weeks here — no mountain holding the world at arm's length, only water in every direction, and a house that has built its whole identity around going out into it rather than standing still against whatever might come up the only road in.*

*Joren is exactly what that water made him, and I could not have known the full shape of it until I had actually watched him inside his own home rather than performing carefully inside ours. He took me out on one of the smaller boats in my second week here — not the grand vessels, nothing built to impress a visiting bride, just an ordinary working craft he plainly knows better than he knows most people — and I understood, watching him handle it, that the restlessness I saw in him at Blackspire is not impatience so much as a kind of hunger he has simply never been permitted to satisfy. He did not talk to me that day about alliances or houses or anything either of our fathers would care to hear repeated. He talked about the Storm Ring, and what he imagines might be past it, and asked me, entirely without calculation, whether I thought a person could actually want something that badly and still be considered sensible.*

*I told him I did not know. I have thought about that question more since than I have thought about almost anything else these two months.*

*I am not accepting him because Vaelmar's alliance matters to our house, though I understand that it does, and I do not resent that truth the way I once feared I might. I am accepting him because two months of actually knowing him have shown me a man who wants something honestly and openly, the way so few people in our own careful world ever permit themselves to want anything aloud — and because somewhere in these two months I found that I wanted, for the first time in longer than I can easily admit, to be looked at the way he looks at the open water. Like something worth the reaching.*

*Tell Selene I am not lost to her. Tell her the sea has letters too, and I intend to use every one of them. Tell Lucian he was right to make me the promise he made, and righter still to have kept it, because I do not think I could have arrived at this honestly any other way.*

*The terms of it all I will leave to wiser heads than mine. I only wanted the four of you to hear the truth of it from me first, in my own words, before it becomes a matter of houses and dates and dowries that has very little left to do with what actually happened here.*

*With all my love, and more certainty than I expected to feel this soon —*
*Lyra*

Vesper set the last page down with the same careful precision he had given the first, and for a long moment no one in the solar spoke at all.

"Well," Orlena said finally, and there was nothing flat in it this time. "I believe that answers rather more than I asked of her."

"She sounds happy," Vesper said, quiet enough that it was not quite meant for the whole room. "I did not fully understand, until just now, how much I had been waiting to hear that specifically, rather than simply the fact of her decision."

Selene had gone very still in the doorway throughout the whole of the reading, and when Lucian found her there afterward, her face carried none of the simple relief he had half expected.

"She said the sea has letters too," Selene said, testing the words as though she did not entirely trust them yet to mean what they seemed to. "That is not the same as her being here."

"No. It is not." Lucian sat beside her rather than crouching this time, an equal rather than a comfort offered from above. "I am not going to tell you this is the same as having her down the hall, because it would be a lie, and I promised you a long time ago I would not do that to you."

"I am glad for her. I want you to know that first, before I say the rest of it, because it is true and I do not want it to sound like it is not." Selene's hands twisted together in her lap. "But I am also allowed to be sad that it means what it means. Both of those things are true at the same time, and I do not think that makes me a bad sister."

"It does not. It makes you an honest one, which is considerably rarer and considerably better." Something eased in Lucian, watching her hold both truths without flinching from either. "What do you want to do with it."

"Write back. Tonight, before anyone else's letter gets there first and tells her everything in the careful, grown-up way that leaves out the parts that actually matter." Selene straightened, some of her old certainty returning to her posture. "I am going to tell her I am happy for her and that I am also sad, in the same letter, in that order, because I think she would rather hear both than have me pretend it is only the first."

"I think she would rather hear exactly that than almost anything else anyone sends her this week."

Selene was already moving toward the writing desk in the corner of the solar before he finished speaking, pulling a fresh sheet toward her with the single-minded determination she gave to very few things beside this, and Lucian watched her begin, unprompted, in a hand still more careful than confident: *Dear Lyra, I am so happy for you, and I also miss you already, and I wanted you to know both of those things before anyone else's letter got there first —*
