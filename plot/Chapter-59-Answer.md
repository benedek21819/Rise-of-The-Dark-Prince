# Chapter 59: Answer

Two months of ordinary business had passed since the morning Lyra's party disappeared around the lower bend of the Ascend, and Lucian had settled, without quite deciding to, into the particular rhythm of a house waiting for something it had stopped mentioning aloud at every meal. The recruits moved steadily through their training, three more names crossed off the list entirely and two others quietly promoted past the point Lucian had once doubted they would reach. Word had come twice already from the operative he had sent south, brief and unremarkable — the party safe, the roads uneventful, nothing requiring more than the report itself.

The letter that finally mattered arrived on an otherwise unremarkable morning, carried into the solar the way every serious piece of correspondence in this house eventually was, and Vesper read it in the particular silence Lucian had come to recognize as the shape of something he was not yet ready to summarize aloud.

"It is from Lyra," he said finally, and something in his voice had eased into a register Lucian rarely heard from him. "She accepts him."

Orlena, seated near the window, did not move for a long moment, and when she spoke there was none of the flat satisfaction Lucian might have expected from a woman who had first raised this question over a year ago. "She says why."

"She says a great many things, most of them addressed more to Lucian and myself than to the substance of the decision itself." Vesper's eyes moved back over the page, and something almost gentle crossed his face as he read it again. "She says she gave herself the two months she asked for, and used every week of it honestly, and that whatever doubts she carried when she left this house did not survive the actual knowing of him. She says she is happy, and asks that we believe her rather than merely hope it on her behalf."

"Then I believe her," Lucian said, before he had fully decided to speak at all, and found, once the words were out, that he meant them entirely.

Selene, who had been listening from the doorway with the particular stillness she reserved for news she suspected mattered before anyone confirmed it did, crossed the room at that and tugged once at her father's sleeve. "Is she coming home."

"Not yet. There are terms still to be settled between our two houses before anything is made formal — matters that concern Vaelmar and Caldarion as much as they concern Lyra and Joren, and neither house does that kind of business hastily, whatever the two of them themselves might prefer." Vesper set the letter down at last, and something in the careful, deliberate way he did it told Lucian the moment had cost him more than his composure let on. "I will write to Ellard today. There is a great deal still to arrange."

"But she said yes." Selene's voice carried the particular flat insistence of a child unwilling to let the larger, more complicated truth crowd out the one fact that actually mattered to her. "That is the important part."

"It is," Vesper agreed, and something in his face, watching her, eased fully for the first time since the letter had been opened. "You are entirely right. Everything else is simply the shape the house has to give it before the rest of Kaelor is permitted to know."

Orlena rose, smoothing her skirts with the particular unhurried precision she brought to every recovery from a moment that had briefly unsettled even her. "Then we had best begin. A wedding between two Great Houses is not arranged in an afternoon, whatever either of these young people might wish were true." Something almost soft moved beneath the practicality of it, gone as quickly as it had surfaced. "I am glad for her. I did not expect to be, quite this much, when I first raised the matter with you both — but I am."

Lucian found himself, once the room had begun to empty into the practical business the news demanded, thinking less about terms or timelines than about the particular ease in his sister's handwriting, visible even secondhand through the plain fact of what she had chosen to say and how she had chosen to say it. Whatever came next — the negotiations, the arrangements, the long work of actually joining two houses rather than merely two people — she had gotten, in the end, exactly what she had asked for from the very first morning this had ever been spoken aloud: the chance to decide for herself, and the room to mean it when she finally did.
