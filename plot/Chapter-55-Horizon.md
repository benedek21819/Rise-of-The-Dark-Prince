# Chapter 55: Horizon

The Vaelmar party crossed the last stretch of open ground with the particular careful pace of travelers who had learned, somewhere on the long climb behind them, not to hurry the final steps of anything approached through Blackspire's mountain. Lord Ellard came first, Lady Meret a half-step beside him rather than behind, and Joren just past his father's shoulder, close enough to the front of the party that no one watching could mistake him for merely accompanying it.

"Lord Vesper." Ellard's bow was correct, exact, the bow of a man who understood precisely how much deference this house was owed and gave no more and no less than that measure. "House Vaelmar thanks you for the honor of this welcome."

"House Caldarion is glad to offer it." Vesper's own inclination of the head was equally measured, two proud houses reading each other across the small, careful distance of the doorway. "The Ascend is unkind to those who do not know it. I trust it did not cost your people too dearly."

"Only our patience, which the sea has already spent teaching us to hold onto more carefully than most." Something almost wry crossed Ellard's weathered face. "A mountain does not frighten men who have spent their lives arguing with open water."

"No," Orlena said, stepping forward with the particular unhurried authority she brought to every room she entered, "I imagine it does not. Lady Meret. It has been some years."

"Longer than either of our houses would likely admit to counting exactly, Lady Orlena." Meret's answering bow carried real warmth beneath its formality. "I am glad the counting has finally stopped mattering enough to be corrected."

Meret's gaze found Lucian next, and something in her appraisal held none of the wariness he had half braced himself for. "So this is the heir I have heard spoken of in rooms considerably less friendly to your house than this one. You are younger than the stories make you sound, Lord Lucian."

"The stories are usually written by people who have never actually stood in a room with me. I find that tends to add years I have not yet earned."

"A fair distinction, and more self-aware than most young lords manage at your age." Something almost approving crossed her weathered face. "I will judge you by the room rather than the stories, then, if you extend my son the same courtesy."

"I intend to extend him exactly the courtesy he earns, no more and no less. I imagine that is all either of us should reasonably ask of the other today."

Joren said nothing yet, and Lucian, watching him from beside his father, understood at once why Selene's household gossip had called him restless — not impatient in any rude or visible way, but present in the particular manner of a man holding himself carefully still, the way water holds itself still just before it is permitted to move. His eyes found Lyra once, briefly, across the width of the hall, and did not linger there longer than courtesy allowed, though something in the effort of that restraint was plain enough to anyone watching for it.

The formal courtesies ran their proper course — travel, weather, the ordinary architecture of two Great Houses being civil to each other in a doorway neither fully trusted the other to have earned. Vesper and Ellard fell, before long, into the particular easy shorthand of men who had each spent a lifetime commanding difficult terrain, discussing shipping lanes and mountain passes as though either subject were entirely free of the weight sitting underneath this whole visit.

"Your Ascend and my own coastline share more in common than most men would guess at first," Ellard said, watching the northern light move across the hall's high windows. "Both punish a man for assuming familiarity is the same thing as safety."

"The difference, I think, is that my mountain has never once changed its mind about what it will allow through," Vesper said. "Your sea, from what little I understand of it, seems to decide fresh each season."

"That is precisely the cruelty of it. A man can learn a mountain's moods in a single lifetime and pass the knowledge down whole. The sea forgets what it taught your grandfather the moment it teaches you something new." Something old and tired moved behind Ellard's eyes, gone as quickly as it surfaced. "I do not say that to complain of my own house's fortune, Lord Vesper. Only to explain why a man raised on that particular unreliability learns to value a mountain's stubbornness rather more than most."

Orlena, meanwhile, drew Meret aside with the practiced skill of a woman who had spent decades making difficult women comfortable enough to speak plainly.

It was Lyra who closed the last of the distance herself, crossing the hall toward Joren without waiting for anyone to arrange the introduction on her behalf.

"Lord Joren," she said. "I understand I am the reason for this particular climb."

"You are." He said it plainly, without the polish Lucian half expected from a man who had spent his letter-writing so carefully. "I could give you a longer answer involving alliances and old grievances between our houses, and it would even be true. But it is not the whole of it, and I did not come all this way to begin our acquaintance with only the part of the truth that sounds well in a hall full of listening ears."

"What is the rest of it, then."

"That I have heard a great deal about you for a long while now, from people with no particular reason to flatter House Caldarion beyond what was actually true, and found myself curious about the woman behind all of it in a way that had nothing to do with what marrying her might do for Vaelmar's fortunes." Something almost unguarded crossed his face, quickly checked but not quickly enough to hide. "I am aware that is not a fashionable thing to admit plainly to a woman I have known for the length of one greeting. I have never been especially good at the fashionable version of anything."

Lyra studied him a moment, the same unhurried, appraising warmth she gave to very little in this house outside her own family. "You are either remarkably honest or remarkably well-rehearsed at sounding it."

"I would rather you decide which, over time, than take my word for it now. Words are cheap from a man asking for your hand. I would rather earn the other kind of proof." He glanced, briefly, toward the tall windows and the grey stretch of sky beyond them, an old habit plainly older than this visit. "For what it is worth, I did not expect the North to be beautiful in this particular way. I have spent my whole life looking at the sea and assuming it was the only thing in Kaelor worth the looking."

"And now?"

"Now I am reconsidering the assumption," Joren said, and did not elaborate further, though the way his gaze returned to her rather than the window made the shape of the rest of it plain enough without needing the words.

"You are very direct, for a man conducting his first real conversation with a woman he hopes to marry."

"I did not see the use in being anything else. Directness has never once cost me a friendship worth keeping, and I doubt it will cost me whatever this becomes either." He held her gaze without any of the practiced ease Lucian half expected from a man raised to court alliances rather than people. "If it does, then I will at least have lost it honestly, which strikes me as considerably better than winning something false."

"That is either the most refreshing thing anyone has said to me in this hall in a year, or the most rehearsed sentence I have heard all morning. I have not yet decided which."

"Then we are already even, since I have spent the whole climb up your mountain wondering precisely the same thing about you."

Across the hall, Selene had held her corner as long as her patience could reasonably be expected to manage, watching the pair of them with the particular fixed, unblinking attention of a child cataloguing every detail she intended to report later in full, before curiosity finally won out over her promise to Lyra entirely. She crossed the distance at something just short of a run and tugged at Lucian's sleeve with the particular urgency of someone carrying news too important to wait.

"Well," she whispered, with the flat, decisive authority of an eleven-year-old passing final judgment. "Marla was right. He is handsome. I do not know yet if he is good, but he is handsome, and I suppose that is a start."

"That is not remotely the measure by which this should be decided."

"I did not say it was the only measure. I said it was a start." She considered the pair of them a moment longer, Lyra and Joren still speaking quietly at the far end of the hall, neither of them in any evident hurry to end it. "She is smiling. Not the polite one. The real one."

Lucian followed her gaze and found, to his own quiet surprise, that she was right.
