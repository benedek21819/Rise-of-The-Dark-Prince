# Chapter 65: Herald

The bond carried it before Lucian had any name for what he was feeling — not the ordinary weather of Ancalion's moods, familiar enough by now to pass through him almost unnoticed, but something sharper, angled outward rather than inward, the particular quality of attention a hound gave a scent rather than a sound. Lucian set down the report he had been reading for Vesper's morning review and went still himself, letting the bond resolve into something he could actually name.

*Another dragon. Distant. Open sky.* The bond offered it wordless and certain, a feeling that arrived with the clarity of speech without ever actually being any.

He was already moving before the thought had fully finished forming — down through the Black Halls, toward the solar, toward his father.

He found Vesper already standing at the tall window rather than at his desk, which told Lucian most of what he needed to know before either of them said a word.

"A dragon is approaching," Lucian said. "From the south."

"I know." Vesper did not turn immediately, and when he did, something in his face held the particular stillness of a man who had felt the same thing through his own bond only moments before and had simply not yet finished deciding what to do with it. "Lirath felt it before you came to tell me. I imagine she has known longer than either of us."

"You think it is him."

"I think we have been waiting for exactly this since the letter arrived, and I would rather be wrong about very little else in my life than about this." Vesper's gaze went back to the window, to the pale southern sky where nothing yet showed itself to an ordinary eye. "Send word to the household. Quietly. I do not want alarm — I want readiness, and I want it to look, from the outside, like nothing has changed here at all."

The next hour moved with the particular controlled economy Lucian had come to recognize as his father's household running exactly as it had been built to run — the Silver Guard repositioning without any visible urgency, staff informed only as much as their roles required, a brief, quiet word sent down to Cazius that put Dawn's own eyes on the southern approach without a single outward sign that anything at all was expected. Lucian stood with his father on the high terrace and watched the sky the way he had once watched it as a boy waiting for nothing in particular, except that now the waiting had a shape, and the shape had a name neither of them had said aloud yet.

The gold came into view first as a point too steady to be anything but deliberate, low against the pale afternoon sky, and grew by degrees into the unmistakable shape of a dragon flying the last exhausted stretch of a very long day. Lucian had never seen Solara before except in Seren's old descriptions — bright, easy comparisons meant to flatter Ancalion rather than describe her honestly — and found, watching her circle once before the final descent, that the reality was both smaller and wearier than anything those descriptions had prepared him for. Her scales caught the light in dulled, travel-worn gold rather than the burnished brightness he had imagined, and her wingbeats, in the last stretch before landing, carried the particular heaviness of a creature asked for more than she had been given in a very long time.

She came down hard on the lower platform, controlled but graceless, and folded her wings with the visible relief of a journey finally ended.

Aldric slid from her back rather than climbed, and stood a moment with one hand still against her flank, gathering himself in a way Lucian understood without needing it explained — the particular toll of a body no longer accustomed to what it had just asked of itself. He looked older than Lucian remembered from the one brief encounter years ago, greyer, the weariness of the flight sitting over an older, deeper weariness that had clearly been there long before he ever climbed onto Solara's back.

"Vesper." He crossed the platform with the careful, deliberate steadiness of a man making sure his legs would hold him. "I would apologize for the manner of my arrival, except that I chose it deliberately, and I find I cannot apologize for a thing I would do again."

"You do not need to apologize for it to me." Vesper met him halfway, and the two men stood a moment in a silence that carried more history than either of them seemed inclined to spend on words. "You look like a man who has not slept properly in a week."

"I have not." Aldric's gaze moved to Lucian, and something in it warmed slightly despite everything else sitting behind it. "Lucian. You have grown considerably since the one occasion I had the pleasure of actually meeting you."

"Your Majesty." Lucian met his eyes directly rather than looking away, courteous without the deference the title might have asked of another house, and found himself studying the King with the same close attention he gave anything that mattered. "Seren speaks of you often."

"She would." Something almost fond crossed Aldric's face before the weight of the day reclaimed it. "I would rather not stand on this platform longer than necessary. What I have to say deserves a room, and I think, given everything, it deserves both of you rather than only Vesper."

Vesper did not question it, and Lucian understood, following them both toward the solar, that whatever line had once existed between what he was told and what he was trusted to carry had quietly finished dissolving sometime in the last several years without either of them ever needing to declare it so.

The solar's doors closed behind them, and Aldric set down, piece by piece, everything he had carried north.

"House Dravenhall has spent years building the largest army on this continent by raw numbers," he said, without preamble, the exhaustion in his voice giving way to something harder and more precise as he spoke. "Not the finest. Large enough that its purpose can no longer be mistaken for caution. My Crown's Eyes have spent years tracing the coin that funded the Corvane operation your own house already broke — it runs, unmistakably now, back to Dravenhall's own coffers, laundered through a banking house too lax to notice what it was carrying. Beside that, what my own people have watched accumulate at Dravenhall's ports and forges for over a year — shipping, iron, grain, at a scale no honest defense requires. And beneath both," he paused, the cost of this part visible even through his composure, "what my own wife gave me without ever asking why I wanted it. Which of her brother's allied houses he has quietly begun drawing men from. How long he has been at it."

The room held a long, settled silence after he finished, and Lucian understood, sitting inside it, that he was hearing for the first time the actual shape of the thing that had been sitting beneath every uneasy letter, every quiet preparation, every careful word since Seren's letter first arrived.

"You came yourself," Vesper said finally, "rather than sending any of that by messenger."

"I judged the risk of interception greater than the risk of being seen." Aldric's hands, folded on the table before him, did not move. "I know what I have done, Vesper. I did not fly here without understanding the cost of flying here."

"Dravenhall has eyes in your own capital. They will have seen you leave." Vesper's voice carried none of the softness he usually reserved for difficult truths, only the flat, unhurried clarity of a man laying out what could no longer be avoided. "Whatever time we thought we had to prepare quietly, we no longer have it. The moment your dragon left Aurelia, Roderic learned his hand was shown, whether he has proof of what you know or only the fact that you moved at all. He will not wait to find out which. This is not a warning of a war that may come. This is very possibly its beginning."

"Yes." Aldric did not flinch from it, did not soften it, only met Vesper's gaze with the plain, unadorned honesty of a man who had already made his peace with the answer long before he ever climbed onto Solara's back. "I knew that when I chose to come. I came anyway, because I judged a war we saw coming, on ground we have already begun preparing, considerably more survivable than one that found us still waiting for proof we might never have gotten in time."

Lucian sat between them, in the particular stillness of a man watching two people he trusted absolutely arrive, independently, at the same unbearable conclusion, and understood that whatever the coming weeks asked of this house, they would not be asking it of a version of him that still needed the shape of the danger explained to him twice.
