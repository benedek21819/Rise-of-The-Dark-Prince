# Chapter 56: Measure

Ellard Vaelmar had the particular gift, Vesper had said to Lucian the night before, of a man who could discuss serious matters while appearing to discuss nothing at all, and Lucian found the truth of it on the second morning of the visit, watching his father and Ellard walk the long gallery overlooking the Ascend with the unhurried pace of men in no evident hurry to reach any conclusion.

"Your mountain keeps its own counsel," Ellard was saying, gesturing out toward the fortified drop of the path below. "I confess I did not expect to admire it. A man who has spent his life arguing with the sea tends to assume nothing on land could truly humble him the same way."

"The Ascend has humbled better men than either of us," Vesper said. "It is the reason this house has never once needed to fear a hostile army reaching its gate. I understand Vaelmar keeps a similar faith in open water — the Storm Ring and the Silent Belt serve you the same purpose the mountain serves me, whatever the cost of losing the old routes through them."

"A cost I think of more often than I let my own house see." Something in Ellard's weathered face eased, briefly, into an honesty Lucian suspected he did not often extend to men outside his own blood. "We were the wealthiest of the Great Houses once, Lord Vesper, before those waters closed against us. I have spent my whole life trying to reach back toward a shore my own grandfather barely remembers clearly. I will admit to you plainly, since you have never once pretended this visit was only about my son's happiness, that a real alliance with Caldarion would matter to Vaelmar in ways considerably larger than one marriage."

"I know it would. I would think less of you if you pretended otherwise." Vesper's own tone carried no judgment in it, only the flat clarity of a man laying his own cards beside another's. "House Caldarion has no need of Vaelmar's navy to defend itself. But a kingdom is not defended by one house's strength alone, whatever this house's reputation suggests to those who have never truly needed our help. An ally with the strongest fleet in Kaelor, bound by more than mere courtesy, is not nothing to us either."

"Then we understand each other's reasons plainly, which is more than most such conversations manage before the wine arrives." Ellard's mouth curved, briefly, into something that was almost warmth. "I would still have my son marry for more than reasons, if the reasons and the wanting can be made to point the same direction. I am not so old a man that I have forgotten there is a difference."

Lucian left them to the rest of it and found, crossing the lower courtyard toward the training yard where he had meant to spend the useful part of his morning, that Joren had beaten him there.

"I hoped I might find you here eventually," Joren said, without preamble, watching Lucian cross the yard with an openness that read as neither calculation nor performance. "I am told this is where the heir of Caldarion actually spends his mornings, whatever else the visitors are shown for the rest of the day."

"Most of it, yes. Did you come to watch, or to ask something."

"Both, if you will permit it. Mostly the second." Joren fell into step beside him, unhurried despite the plain restlessness Lucian had already marked in him the day before. "I am not fool enough to think I need your permission for anything regarding your sister. But I am not fool enough, either, to think her brother's opinion of me carries no weight in this house, whatever the formal shape of the decision ends up being. I would rather earn something honest from you than simply wait to learn afterward whether I had it."

"That is a more direct approach than most men manage with me."

"I have never been particularly good at the indirect kind. Ask my father — it has cost me more patience with him than either of us would like to admit." Something almost rueful crossed Joren's face. "I would rather you judge me for what I actually am than for whatever careful version of myself I could perform for a single afternoon and then fail to sustain."

Lucian considered him a moment, the same unhurried weighing he had learned to give every name that crossed the table in the windowless room off the lower solar. "Then I will ask you plainly, since you have asked for exactly that. Why her. Not the alliance. Her."

"Because everything I had heard of her before I ever wrote that letter made her sound like someone worth actually knowing, and everything I have seen since arriving has only confirmed it." Joren held his gaze without flinching from the directness of the question. "I will not pretend the alliance means nothing to my house, because you would not believe me if I did, and you would be right not to. But I did not choose to feel curious about her. I simply did, and I have spent every hour since arriving here glad that I was right to."

"And if she says no."

"Then I will have lost something I wanted, and I will go home and be honest with my father about why, and Vaelmar will have to find its alliance some other way." Joren said it plainly, without any visible attempt to soften it into something more strategic. "I did not come here to force an answer out of her, Lord Lucian. I came because she asked to actually meet me before deciding, and I thought that deserved an honest man showing up for it, whatever the eventual cost to my own house's ambitions."

Something in the flat sincerity of it settled a portion of the wariness Lucian had been carrying since the letter first arrived, though he kept his own face carefully even. "You spar."

"Badly, by Caldarion standards, I imagine. Vaelmar trains for a deck, not a mountain." A real grin broke across Joren's face, sudden and unguarded. "I would still like to try, if you are willing to be patient with how much of it I get wrong."

They found their way to the practice ring before the morning was much older, and Lucian found, working through the first careful exchanges with a man whose footing kept betraying a lifetime spent compensating for rolling water rather than solid stone, that he did not mind the patience it asked of him nearly as much as he had expected to.

Lyra found them there sometime past midday, and did not bother hiding the fact that she had come looking. "I am told you have been testing him," she said to Lucian, watching Joren catch his breath with the particular good humor of a man who had lost more exchanges than he had won and seemed entirely unbothered by the count.

"A little."

"And your verdict."

"I do not have one yet. I am still weighing it." Lucian glanced at Joren, who had straightened and was watching the two of them with an open curiosity that made no effort to disguise itself as anything else. "I will tell you honestly when I do."

"That is all I have ever asked of you," Lyra said, and something in the ease between the three of them, standing there in the ordinary afternoon light of a yard that had never once before hosted a stranger with this much of the house's attention on him, felt less like the beginning of a negotiation than the beginning of something none of them had fully named yet.
