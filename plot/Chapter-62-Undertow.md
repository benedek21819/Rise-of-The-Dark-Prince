# Chapter 62: Undertow

The letters from Aurelia arrived with a regularity Lucian had long since stopped remarking on, and he found this one waiting on his desk the way he found most of them now — set aside by Cole with the same unhurried care given to any correspondence, left for whatever hour of the day Lucian actually got around to it rather than whatever hour it deserved. He turned it over once before opening it, recognizing the seal, and felt the small, familiar twinge of something between guilt and fondness that her letters had come, over the years, to carry for him without his ever quite deciding it should be that way.

*Lucian —*

*I have started counting how many words your last three letters contained, combined, and I will not tell you the number because I do not think either of us would enjoy hearing it aloud. I am not writing this to scold you. I have simply reached the age where I have decided scolding you accomplishes nothing beyond making you write even less, out of what I can only assume is some private, stubborn Caldarion principle about not rewarding complaint with more of yourself than the complaint already extracted.*

*Aurelia is much the same as it always is, which I have come to find comforting rather than tedious, the older I get. Corvin has taken to reminding everyone at every possible opportunity that he outranks me in some technical genealogical sense I have never fully understood and do not intend to start understanding now. Nerissa has grown taller than is strictly fair to the rest of us. I still find the overlook most mornings before the palace properly wakes, the same one I found you at once, years ago now, though you have never once come back to it since, which I choose to take as evidence you are simply too far away rather than evidence of anything less flattering.*

*Father has been strange lately. Not unkind, only strange — distracted in the particular way he gets when something is weighing on him that he has decided the rest of us are not yet meant to carry. He has been talking, more than once this month, about wanting to see the North himself, properly, the way he apparently has not in years — he will not say why, only that it has become important to him rather suddenly, and I probably should not be telling you that, since I do not think he has said it to anyone outside the family yet. I cannot seem to help myself where you are concerned, though. I never could.*

*Write back properly this time. Or do not, and I will simply assume you are too busy becoming whatever it is you are becoming, and love you regardless, because apparently that is simply the shape my own stubborn principle has taken.*

*Yours, still and always —*
*Seren*

Lucian read it twice through before the third paragraph settled into something other than ordinary court gossip, and by the second reading he found he could not entirely dismiss the small, cold certainty gathering beneath it. Aldric did not simply decide, on private whim, to visit a duchy he had not personally set foot in for years. And Seren did not, whatever her own guileless account of herself, let slip anything she had not been given at least tacit permission to let slip.

He found his father in the solar within the hour, the letter still in hand.

"Read the third paragraph," Lucian said, setting it on the table between them without further preamble.

Vesper read it once, in the same unhurried silence he gave any serious correspondence, and Lucian watched something settle behind his eyes well before he finished — not surprise exactly, but the particular stillness of a man confirming a shape he had already half suspected rather than encountering one for the first time.

"He is coming here," Vesper said, when he had finished. "Personally. And he has chosen to tell us so through his daughter's ordinary correspondence rather than through any formal channel, which means he does not trust his formal channels with whatever this actually is."

"You do not think this is simply a father's passing whim, relayed carelessly by a daughter who does not know better."

"I think Aldric has never once done anything carelessly in the whole of his reign, however much his own daughter may genuinely believe otherwise." Vesper set the letter down with the same careful precision he gave every piece of correspondence that had cost him more than its length suggested. "A princess writing to her betrothed about her father's restlessness is nothing. A King's own message to this house, sent through any channel that could be traced back to him, is considerably more than nothing to whoever might be watching for exactly that. If he wanted us warned without wanting anyone else to know he had given the warning, this is precisely how a careful man would choose to give it."

"Warned of what."

"I do not know the particulars yet. I know the shape of it well enough." Something in Vesper's face had gone quiet in the particular way it did before he asked something difficult of himself. "Whatever it is, Lucian, a king does not cross this kingdom in person, on a whim wrapped inside his own daughter's letter, for anything small enough to wait for the ordinary post. You know already where my own suspicion has sat since the leak two years ago. What neither of us has ever had is proof enough to act on it. If Aldric is coming himself, he may finally have found what we could not produce on our own." He was quiet a moment. "The letter itself is not the danger. It is only warning of the danger to come. And if he means to reach us with the urgency this implies, he will not trust it to the roads — Solara makes the distance in days rather than weeks, and a king who has decided this cannot wait will not choose the slower road simply for the sake of caution. The moment he is seen leaving the capital, whoever moves against us will know they have been found out, and will not wait politely to confirm it. However long we have, it likely begins shrinking the day he takes to the sky, not the day he lands here."

Lucian looked at the letter again, at Seren's easy, unguarded hand, the small private joke about the overlook sitting only a few lines above the one that had just reshaped the whole of his afternoon, and found that he did not know, reading it a third time, whether to be more unsettled by what it might mean or by how easily she had carried something this heavy inside something that still, underneath it all, read exactly like herself.

"Write back to her," Vesper said, watching him. "Whatever else this turns out to be, I suspect she will have earned a great deal from you before this is finished."

"I intend to."

"Good." Vesper rose, already turning the shape of the coming days over behind his composed face. "In the meantime, we prepare quietly, and we wait for whatever the King himself judges too dangerous to trust to anyone else."

Lucian sat with the letter a long while after his father had gone, in the particular stillness of a man weighing how to answer something that had turned out to matter considerably more than the hour he had first set aside for it. He thought of the overlook she still visited, alone, years after the one morning he had happened to find it first. He thought of the last several replies, short enough to count their own words against, and what it must have cost her, given how plainly she still loved being heard, to keep sending the longer kind regardless.

*Seren —*

*I counted the words myself, once you mentioned it, and you are right that neither of us would enjoy the number aloud. I will not defend it. I will only tell you that the shortness was never about you, whatever it looked like from your side of it, and that I mean to do better, starting now, because you have earned considerably better than what I have been giving you.*

*I still remember the overlook. I have simply never found a morning quiet enough, since, to go looking for it the way you do. Perhaps that will change, before too much longer.*

*Tell your father, if you find the occasion for it, that whatever brings him north will find this house ready to receive him properly, whatever shape "properly" turns out to need to take.*

*Write again soon. I intend to actually answer it this time, at the length it deserves.*

*— Lucian*

He sealed it before he could talk himself into starting over on something shorter and more carefully guarded, the kind of letter that had become his habit without his quite noticing when it had, and found, handing it to Cole for the morning courier, that he did not regret a single unshortened line of it.

Selene found him in the corridor afterward, and studied his face with the particular sharpness she had never once lost since she was small enough to be told, gently, that some things were not hers to ask about. "You have the face again. Not the arithmetic one. The other one."

"Which other one."

"The one you had before the trouble with House Corvane, all those years ago. I was too young to understand it properly then. I understand it well enough now." She settled, plainly satisfied with having placed it correctly. "The one that means something is coming that you have not decided how to feel about yet."

"Something may be coming," Lucian allowed. "I do not know what, yet. I will tell you honestly when I do, the same as I have always told you."

"I know you will." She considered him a moment longer, then reached up and straightened his collar with an unconscious echo of a gesture she had watched Lyra make a hundred times before her, apparently absorbed into her own hands without her ever meaning to learn it. "Whatever it is, I hope it is smaller than your face is currently telling me it is."

"So do I," Lucian said, and found, walking on, that he meant it more than he entirely wanted to admit.
