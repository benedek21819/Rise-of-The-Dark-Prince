# Chapter 48: Care

Vesper listened the way he always listened, without interrupting, and Lucian gave him the whole of it — the trap's unexpected surfacing inside the Crown's own channels, Isolde's offer, and the exchange that had, in Lucian's own judgment, done more to earn her a hearing than anything else said in that plain, unremarkable room.

"You asked her outright whether her brother's blood made her word worth less."

"I did not see a way to weigh anything she offered honestly without asking it first." Lucian held his father's gaze. "She did not flinch from it. I do not know if that proves her honest. I know it proved she has answered the question herself, many times, long before I arrived to ask it of her."

Vesper was quiet for a long moment, turning it the way he turned anything too large to accept or refuse in a single breath. "A queen born Dravenhall, offering House Caldarion a hand against her own brother's ambitions. It is either the most valuable alliance this house has been offered in a generation, or the single most elegant piece of misdirection Dravenhall has ever attempted against us — and I do not think even she could tell you, with total certainty, which one it will prove to be a year from now, whatever she believes of herself today."

"You do not trust her."

"I do not trust anyone I have not watched be tested, Lucian, and neither should you, however plainly she spoke to you in that room." Vesper rose, crossed to the window, the same habit Lucian had watched him fall into again and again these last weeks whenever a decision required the particular stillness only the grey northern light seemed to give him. "But I do not need to trust her fully to use what she is offering carefully. We will not hand the Crown's service the truth of our leak, nor the shape of what we have already narrowed. We will give her something smaller — verifiable, useful, costing us little if it is wasted — and see, patiently, whether what she gives us in return is worth the same weight she claims it carries. If it is, we go further. If it is not, we have lost nothing beyond a single small truth that was never going to matter much on its own."

"The same doctrine as the intermediary in Firsthold."

"The same doctrine this house has used for longer than either of us has been alive to remember choosing it." Something almost like approval crossed Vesper's face. "You brought back more than a message, Lucian. You brought back a woman's measure, taken plainly and honestly, in a room built for exactly the opposite. That is worth more to me today than anything she actually promised."

"What do I tell her, if she asks for more before we are ready to give it?"

"Tell her the truth. That House Caldarion moves carefully with everyone, queens included, and that patience has served this house considerably longer than trust ever has." Vesper's mouth eased, very slightly, into something that was not quite a smile. "I imagine, from what you have told me of her, that she will respect that answer a great deal more than she would have respected an eager one."

He left the solar sometime after, the weight of the last hour still sitting close beneath his ribs the way it always did after a conversation that asked this much of him, and found himself walking not toward his own rooms but toward the quieter wing of the keep where his mother kept her hours, without entirely deciding to go there until he had already arrived.

Elara was in the small sunlit room she had claimed for herself years before Lucian had words for why a room might need claiming — bright even on a grey northern afternoon, warm in a way the rest of Blackspire rarely allowed itself to be, a half-finished piece of needlework abandoned in her lap in favor of the book she was actually reading. She looked up when he entered, and something in her face did what it always did when she looked at him properly for the first time after any absence — searched, quietly, for whatever the day had cost him before either of them said a single word about it.

"You flew somewhere you did not tell me about," she said, setting the book aside entirely. "Your father's face, when he thinks no one is reading it, gives away considerably more than he believes it does."

"Aurelia. A private matter." He sat across from her, in the chair that had, without either of them ever quite deciding it, become his over the years. "I cannot tell you the whole of it."

"I did not ask for the whole of it." She studied him for a longer moment than the words alone accounted for — the sword now permanently at his side where once there had been none, something settled and older in the set of his shoulders that had not been there the last time she had looked at him this closely. "I asked because I wanted to see your face while you told me what you could, and because I find, the older you become, that your face is the only report I am still permitted to read in full."

Something in him eased at that, the particular unguarded loosening he allowed almost nowhere else in this house. "It has been a difficult season, Mother."

"I know. I have watched it happen to you from close enough to count every piece of it, even the pieces no one has explained to me directly." She reached across the small distance between their chairs and took his hand, the way she had taken it when he was small enough that the gesture required no explanation at all. "I am not going to ask you to set any of it down simply because you are sitting in my sunlight for an hour. I know better than that by now. I am only going to ask you to sit in it a while longer before you go back to carrying the rest."

He stayed longer than he had meant to, the grey afternoon light shifting slowly across the room's small warm clutter, Elara asking nothing further and offering, instead, the easy, undemanding company of a mother content simply to have her son within reach — a question about Selene's latest scheme involving the hawk on the eastern wall, a quiet remark about how strange it still felt to see Vesper's own blade at his hip, a comfortable silence neither of them felt any particular need to fill. It was, Lucian thought, watching the light move without any of the urgency the rest of his days had carried in a very long time, the first hour since the crossing that had asked absolutely nothing of him at all.

"Thank you," he said eventually, rising, "for not asking."

"I am your mother, Lucian." Elara looked up at him with the same soft, unhurried certainty she had worn his entire life, the one thing in this house that had never once changed shape around whatever else he had become. "Asking was never the part of loving you that mattered most."
