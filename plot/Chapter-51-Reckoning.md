# Chapter 51: Reckoning

The Great Hall of Aurelia held eight houses beneath its banners for the first time in longer than any single person present could claim to remember, and Lucian understood, taking his place at his father's side, that the room's true business had already begun before a single word of it was spoken — in who sat near whom, in which banners had been arranged to face which, in the particular careful distance the ushers had left between Caldarion's black and silver and Dravenhall's own colors across the width of the floor.

King Aldric rose from a throne that seemed, for the length of this gathering, less a seat of comfort than a place a man stood watch from. Isolde sat beside him, composed and unreadable, and Lucian caught her eyes only once, briefly, before the hall's attention gathered itself toward the throne and away from anything so small as a single glance.

"I will not waste the patience of eight houses pretending this gathering is only tradition," Aldric said, and something in the plain weight of his opening line settled the hall's low murmur into full silence faster than any herald's call could have. "You have each, in your own duchies, watched a slow thing happen for longer than any of us have found convenient to name aloud. Irregularities in the Veil — small, scattered, explainable one at a time, for decades. A strange season here. An old wives' account of lights over water there. None of it, alone, worth troubling a Convocation over." He paused, and let the silence that followed carry its own weight. "What has changed is not that these things have begun. It is their shape over time. Frequency and severity, both climbing, not gently, across the years I have kept records of them personally. I have had scholars set the curve against itself, decade against decade, and I will tell you plainly that the picture it paints, carried forward honestly, is not one any house in this hall can afford to keep treating as scattered coincidence."

"With respect, Your Majesty." Halvard Kestrion did not wait to be formally recognized, and something in the flat, controlled violence of his voice told Lucian this was not a man accustomed to waiting for permission to speak about whatever had brought him to his feet. "Some of us are no longer speaking in decades or curves. My deepest Deepcoal seams have begun killing my own people without any cause my engineers can name — fires with no spark, ground that has held a century giving way without warning. I did not come to this hall for a lecture on trajectories. I came because my house is bleeding now, and I want to know what is doing it."

"Then you will have your answer sought as urgently as any house's," Aldric said, and did not flinch from the bluntness. "Lord Kestrion's account is the sharpest example any house has yet brought before this Crown. It will not be the only one heard today."

Lord Ellard Vaelmar rose next, more measured than Halvard had been, though Lucian did not miss the careful control beneath his own composure — a man choosing his words the way a gambler counts coin before laying it down. "House Vaelmar has watched the waters east of Kaelor for longer than any house's records reach back cleanly. I will not stand here and manufacture an irregularity to match the urgency this hall clearly wants — the dangers there remain precisely what they have long been, cruel and consistent, nothing in my own captains' reports that marks any real change." Something flickered behind his composed delivery, gone before it could be named plainly as disappointment. "But I would ask this Crown to remember, when the search for a cause begins in earnest, that no house has a longer or more direct stake in what lies behind those dangers than mine. Whatever answer this Convocation eventually finds, I would have Vaelmar included in the finding of it."

A shorter silence followed Roderic Dravenhall's own account, delivered standing but brief, offering little beyond confirmation that Dravenhall's own lands had recorded similar irregularities without Kestrion's or Vaelmar's urgency behind them — and Lucian noticed, watching him, the particular careful economy of a man who had chosen to say precisely as much as courtesy required and not a single word more, his gaze crossing Vesper's exactly once before returning, deliberately, to the throne.

It was Magister Emeric Myrath who bore the weight of what came after, called forward by name rather than allowed to volunteer, and Lucian watched him field question after question — from Halvard directly, sharper each time the answers came back careful rather than certain — with the particular strain of a man caught between genuine scholarship and a house doctrine that had never once required him to commit to anything so publicly before.

"House Myrath maintains extensive records of Veil phenomena across the continent's history," Emeric said, choosing each word with visible care. "What we do not maintain, and what no house in this hall currently possesses, is a confirmed cause. I can tell you the pattern is real. I can tell you it has precedent, faint and old, in records even my own house has not fully reconciled. I cannot yet tell you what is producing it, and I would be doing every house in this room a disservice if I pretended otherwise simply to give an anxious hall the comfort of an answer."

"Convenient," Halvard said, low, though not quite low enough to go unheard.

"Convenient would be telling you what you want to hear, Lord Kestrion," Emeric said, and something steadier entered his voice for the first time since he had been called forward. "I am telling you what I actually know, which is considerably less, and considerably more honest."

Vesper rose when the hall's attention finally, inevitably, turned toward the banner it had spent the entire session pointedly not yet asking to speak, and Lucian felt something in the room's collective posture shift — not hostility exactly, but the particular sharpened attention of an audience that had waited the whole performance for its actual subject to finally take the stage.

"House Caldarion's Northern Duchy has recorded its own irregularities," Vesper said, plain and controlled, giving the hall precisely as much as the moment required and nothing that was not already true. "Wild creatures larger and more aggressive than any generation before them, in numbers that have made our own culling hunts more frequent with each passing decade rather than less. We have no explanation beyond theory, and no theory confirmed enough to offer this hall as fact rather than speculation." He let that settle, unhurried, before continuing. "I did not attend this Convocation to withhold what my house has observed. I attended because I was asked to, plainly and personally, and I have given this hall exactly what I would give it regardless of how that invitation was worded."

Lucian felt the weight of the room's attention brush past his father and settle, briefly, on him instead — the heir finally present after a generation of Caldarion's empty seat — and it was in that same charged moment, with a hall full of half-understood accounts of the Veil's wrongness pressing in from every direction at once, that something in him answered without being asked to. Not the Absence, not fully — nothing that touched another person's senses at all. Only his own, sharpening for the length of a single unbidden breath, the hall's every small sound suddenly separated and legible around him, the particular cold clarity he had learned, across a year of training, to recognize and fear in equal measure.

It passed as quickly as it had come, and he was grateful, in the same breath, that no one in that crowded hall had been watching him closely enough to notice.

"This Convocation will not resolve what has taken decades to build in a single session," Aldric said, once the last house had spoken and the hall's murmur had begun, unbidden, to rise again beneath the weight of everything just laid bare. "But it will not disperse pretending nothing was said here either. I am forming a standing council of this Convocation's own choosing, drawn from houses willing to commit real resources to finding what the rest of us can only theorize about. I will hear who is willing before this session closes, and I promise you all plainly: whatever this is, it does not wait for our comfort while we decide how urgently to take it."

Lucian looked out over the hall — Halvard's barely-contained fury, Ellard's careful hope, Emeric's exhausted honesty, his own father's controlled stillness beside him — and understood, sitting inside a room finally forced to reckon with something it had spent decades finding easier to ignore, that whatever answer eventually came would not arrive gently, and would very likely cost more than anyone in this hall yet understood themselves to be offering.
