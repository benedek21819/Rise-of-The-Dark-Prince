# Chapter 71: Fall

He could not put it down.

That was the thing Vesper kept coming back to, in the small part of himself still capable of coming back to anything. His hands had stopped being his some while ago. He had lost the feeling in them, then the shaking, which had seemed like an improvement until he understood what it meant. His breath was going in and out too fast and too shallow to be of any use to him and would not slow no matter what he did about it.

And none of it mattered, because the moment he let the field go, Korrath would be fast.

So he held it, and watched the sixth pass go in above him, and let himself have one clean unguarded second of something that was very close to hope.

Then Roderic Dravenhall put his hand inside his coat and took something out of it.

Vesper saw it. He did not hear anything — there were two hundred feet of wind and two dragons between them, and whatever was said up there was never going to reach the bridge he was standing on.

---

Lirath stopped flying.

Not fell. That was the thing, and Vesper's mind fixed on it with a terrible clarity while the rest of him was still catching up: **she did not fall.** Falling is what happens when something breaks. Her wings did not break. They did not fold or falter or tear. They simply stopped being hers — went slack in the middle of a beat, the way a hand opens when its owner stops meaning to hold something — and everything else about her went on working perfectly and answered nothing at all.

She went over sideways and down past the cliff face with her eyes open.

Vesper understood it in the time it took her to clear the first hundred feet, and he did not need to be told and there was nobody to tell him.

Dragons do not do that. There is no wound that does it, no working, no poison, nothing in the whole of the Veil that reaches into a living will and takes it out of her.

One thing does that, and Vesper knew the name of it, and had known since the year they gave him this house and everything under it.

House Dravenhall were kings on this continent before there was a crown in Aurelia to argue with.

Kings keep things.

That was all of it. It took no longer than that, and then he stopped thinking entirely and watched her go down.

---

She fought it near the ground.

He saw it happen — saw whatever had hold of her run out or let go or simply finish, four hundred feet above the lower slopes, and saw her come back into herself all at once and try. Wings out. Too late and too fast and at the wrong angle, but out, and hauling, and turning what would have been a straight vertical death into something with a shallow to it.

She hit the scree on the eastern shoulder and went down it a long way in a slide of stone and snow and came to rest against the rocks at the bottom, and did not move, and then moved.

Alive.

Half a mile below him and across two ridges, on ground with no path to it, alive, and there was no more chance of his reaching her today than of his reaching the sea.

---

There was never any question about what came next.

Roderic had not marched the length of a continent for a keep. Blackspire is a house and a mine and a road, and not one of those is what has kept the North out of other men's hands for two hundred years. He had come for the two things that were. One of them was lying on the eastern scree with her wing under her.

Korrath came around, low and laboring, hard into the wind, and went at Ancalion — not because Ancalion mattered to him, but because Ancalion was in the way.

And Vesper watched it with the field still open at full pressure and understood something he had somehow not understood in twenty-six years of carrying this.

*It is not enough.*

It was working. It was working perfectly — the animal was hauling himself through the air at three times his own weight and every turn cost him twice what it should have, and it did not matter, because he no longer had two dragons to solve. He had one, and it was torn through the wing and slowing, and Vesper could see from the ground how much it was slowing. Full pressure. Correctly applied. Everything Vesper Caldarion had ever been able to do with the thing his family gave him, held at its absolute limit until his body was failing under it.

And Korrath was still winning.

---

Then Korrath came at the bridge.

Vesper watched it start and knew what it was before the turn finished, because it was the correct play and he would have made it himself. Roderic was inside the field. Roderic had been inside it for an hour and knew exactly what was being done to him, and knew a thing like that has a source, and there was a man standing entirely alone in the open on a stone bridge two hundred feet below the fight, not moving, not fighting, not doing anything at all.

Kill him and it stops. Kill him and Korrath is fast in the same instant, and the black one dies inside a minute, and there is nothing left on this mountain but a household and a road.

It was not a shortcut to what Roderic wanted. It *was* what Roderic wanted, and it happened to also be the quickest way to the rest of it.

"My lord." Valtor was at his shoulder, and had his arm, and Valtor had never once in nineteen years put a hand on him. "*My lord* —"

Vesper did not answer him. He could not have spared the breath.

He could go. That was the thing. Twelve running paces to the arch and the rock over his head, and Valtor would get him there, and he would live.

And the field would close as he moved, and Korrath would be whole again, and his son would be eight hundred feet up on a wing that was already torn, alone, against something that would not need thirty seconds.

So he did not go.

He stood on the bridge in the open with his arms shaking and his breath coming like a man drowning, and he held it, and he let them come at him. He had done many hard things in the running of this house and had thought he understood what they cost. It turned out that all of them had involved doing something.

Above him — very fast, out of nothing, from the wrong side — a black shape came down across the front of Korrath's turn and broke it.

Vesper closed his eyes for one second. Then he opened them and held on.

---

The mountain made a sound.

It came up through the stone under his boots before it reached his ears, and he knew it the way a man knows a voice out of his own childhood.

He had not heard it in twenty-six years.

Valtor's grip went off his arm.

Somewhere behind and below the roosts, in the deep places where nobody goes and nothing has gone in twenty-six years, something very large and very old had stopped being asleep.

Vesper did not turn around. He did not need to look, and he had nothing left over for looking.

He knew what it was. He knew, too, precisely why — because he was the only man on this mountain who knew both halves of it: what was down there, and what had just been done, in front of it, to the one thing in the world it had ever come out of the dark for.

Not rescue. Nobody was rescuing anybody.

Answer.
