# World Overview

## The World — Kaelor and Arcanor

**The world has no name in common use, and that is deliberate.** Nobody living on Kaelor has any practical reason to name the whole world, because they cannot reach the rest of it. There are two continents that matter: **Arcanor**, the old imperial mainland in the east, and **Kaelor**, the remote western continent where this story takes place. For nearly everyone alive, one of those is the world and the other is a rumour. *(Available to name later if a scene ever needs it — nothing depends on it.)*

**This is not a world shattered by ancient war, and its gods are not silent — it has none.** It is a world in slow, quiet decline from **a single specific wound**: a seal that is failing, and has been failing for two and a half centuries, because the family that held it was driven away. Almost nobody understands that. They only see the results — seas that can no longer be crossed, a continent going bad at the edges, weather and wildlife that get worse by the generation — and they explain them however they can.

**Two continents, in opposite conditions:**

- **Arcanor** — the old continent, seat of a fallen empire that once held thirteen kingdoms. Now blighted and dangerous, worst around the abandoned imperial capital where the Dark Throne still stands empty. Ground where little grows cleanly, creatures worse than the land ever made, places where the Veil sits thin enough to feel. Still populated, still politically alive, still home to the Sisters of the Current and to old families that have not forgotten what was lost — but visibly decaying.
- **Kaelor** — remote, isolated, and whole. A single kingdom of nine duchies under eight Great Houses, compact enough to cross in a couple of weeks. Spared the decay entirely, for one reason only: distance. It sits far enough from the wound that the wound has not reached it. That protection is not permanent and nobody has earned it.

**Between them: an ocean that closed.** The Silent Belt, the Storm Ring and the Outer Deep were crossable when the exile happened. They are not now. Kaelor's isolation is not geography — it is a symptom.

**Tone of the World:** Dark, atmospheric, politically treacherous, with pockets of beauty and hope. Cold and restrained on the surface, with sudden decisive violence and real tenderness underneath. Morally grey rather than good against evil — see `plot/logline-and-synopsis.md` for the full tone and theme statement.

**Technology Level:** Medieval. Steel, siege engines, sail and horse, no gunpowder. What changes the arithmetic is not machinery but **dragons** — of which fewer than a handful remain on Kaelor — and **Cold Silver**, which exists in one mountain and belongs to one house.

**Magic Prevalence:** Real, known, and **gated by bloodline**. Veilweaving is not folklore or heresy — it is studied openly, House Myrath keeps archives on it, and anyone can watch it work. But it takes Will and inherited affinity, so it belongs overwhelmingly to old families, and it is genuinely dangerous to its user: **Veilrot** is permanent and everyone knows it. There is no priesthood and no doctrine, because a thing you can test does not need faith — Kaelor got **scholarship where another world would have got a church** (see `history-and-lore.md`, "Faith and Oaths in Kaelor").

Three distinct powers exist, and none becomes another: **the Veil**, **Commandments**, and **the Primordial Darkness** — see `magic-and-power-system.md`.

## Core World Concept

A man once forced the Darkness back through a tear in the sky, and the world knelt and made his family emperors for it. They held the seal for more than eight centuries. Then a warning was misread into a threat, and they were driven out — and nothing has held it since. The seas turned, a continent began to rot, and the world has been quietly dying ever since without ever understanding why, because the truth is only that a chair is empty. The last of that line now holds a mountain at the far edge of the only continent still whole, and has spent two and a half centuries making very sure that nobody remembers who they are. Their heir is eighteen, colder than his house intended, bonded to a dragon born from the same darkness his ancestors mastered — and the world is about to start remembering.
