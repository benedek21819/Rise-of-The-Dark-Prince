## Key Historical Events (Chronological)

### The True History of Kaelor (Known Only to the Heads of Both Houses)
Hundreds of years ago, an emperor of House Caldarion granted the continent of Kaelor to House Kaelarion with full autonomous rule. At the time, House Kaelarion was a loyal vassal house of the Caldarion Dynasty. House Kaelarion has ruled Kaelor publicly ever since.

Kaelor was not empty or ownerless before that grant. It was ruled independently by the ancestors of House Dravenhall, who had never sworn fealty to the Caldarion Empire and had no part in its founding or its centuries of expansion. When the emperor's decision arrived, backed by the authority of the largest empire in the world, Dravenhall submitted peacefully rather than resist what it had no realistic hope of opposing — no battle, no siege, no defeat to point to and blame, only the quiet, total loss of sole rule over the land its family had always called its own. It became one Great House among several rather than Kaelor's master. The grievance outlived the transition by centuries, sharpened rather than dulled by having no clear villain except history itself — and neither Dravenhall nor anyone else on Kaelor has ever known that the decision came from an empire whose last living fragment now sits quietly in the Northern Duchy, closer to them than they have ever imagined.

Centuries later, an ancient prophecy about a coming doom was misinterpreted by the people of the old continent (Arcanor). They came to believe that the Caldarion Dynasty would be the cause of the disasters. Under pressure from the people, the emperor of that time accepted the will of the people and voluntarily entered exile with his family, leaving the old continent behind.

#### The Prophecy
The words survive, or most of them do: *"When the Dark Throne is forsaken, the world shall remember what it was made to forget."* It is attributed, in what fragments remain, to an ancient Veilseer of the Sisters of the Current — unnamed now, lost to the same centuries that lost almost everything else about the empire's founding age.

Read as it was meant, it is a warning, not a threat: *forsaken* means left empty, abandoned by its guardian, and the sentence says plainly that if the throne ever goes unheld, the danger it was built to contain returns. It is, in essence, the same truth the seas and the blighted stretches of Arcanor itself have since proven true many times over — the throne's occupant is not the danger. Their absence is.

It was never read that way by the people who mattered. Two words carried the misreading. *Forsaken* slid easily, in frightened mouths, from something that happens to a throne into something people do to it — renounced, rejected, cast off, rather than left empty. Once forsaken became an action rather than a condition, *the world shall remember what it was made to forget* stopped sounding like the return of an old danger and started sounding like a promised awakening: the world finally seeing clearly, once the throne — and the visibly shadow-touched house sitting on it — was finally gone. Read that way, the prophecy did not warn against losing the throne's guardian. It promised relief once the guardian was thrown off.

The misreading was not purely innocent. Factions within the empire who had no realistic path to unseating House Caldarion by force found the ambiguity extremely useful, and did nothing to correct it — some helped it spread. Fear did the rest. By the time the secret Caldarion egg appeared around Year 800, intensifying a suspicion that had already been building for generations, the corrected reading had no chance of being heard over the convenient one. The exile that followed was meant to avert the very disaster the true prophecy said it would cause. It did exactly that, precisely because everyone acting on it had the warning backward.

The Caldarion family chose Kaelor as their new home because it was remote, extremely difficult to access, and already ruled by their loyal vassal house. As a result, **House Kaelarion is technically a vassal to House Caldarion**, even though this truth has been forgotten by almost everyone.

House Kaelarion was the very first house to kneel on the day of the First Eclipse, before the empire had a name or a throne to grant. Every other house that knelt beside them that day belonged to Arcanor, and stayed there when the Caldarions left. Once the seas closed behind the exile, those older vassal ties became unreachable rather than broken — still real, in principle, but severed by an ocean no ship could cross. House Kaelarion is not merely *a* vassal of the fallen empire. Centuries after being the first house to kneel, it is now the only one Caldarion can still reach, protect, or call upon — the first and the last, with everything the empire once ruled between them lost to distance and time.

At the moment it knelt, it was nothing to speak of — a minor, barely-surviving house with no name worth remembering beyond its own small holdings, possessing nothing that distinguished it except having been present, and having chosen loyalty, in the instant the world changed. That single act bought it nothing immediate. What it bought, across the more than six hundred years that followed, was trust — accumulated slowly, generation after generation, through service the empire came to rely on and loyalty it never had cause to doubt. By the time an emperor of House Caldarion looked for a house steady enough to hand an entire continent to, the choice was not a reward for one old act of kneeling. It was the sum of six centuries spent proving that the kneeling had meant something. Only then, with a continent and a throne finally its own, did the house set aside the small, forgotten name it had carried since before the empire existed and take the name it has held ever since — Kaelarion, built from Kaelor itself, binding what it had become to the land the empire had finally trusted it to hold.

When the Caldarions first arrived on Kaelor, the oceans surrounding the continent were still navigable with conventional means. However, in their absence from Arcanor, the loss of their control over the primordial darkness on the other side of the Veil caused a gradual escalation of dangers at sea. Over the course of many decades, the no-wind zones expanded, violent storms became more frequent and severe, and monstrous creatures began appearing in greater numbers. What was once difficult but possible travel slowly became nearly impossible.

The seal's failure was never only a maritime problem — the Primordial Darkness presses against the land as readily as the water, and Arcanor itself, closest to the empty Dark Throne, has borne the worst of it. Wild stretches of the old continent have grown genuinely dangerous over the centuries since the exile: blighted ground where little grows cleanly, creatures more monstrous than the land ever produced before, and pockets where the Veil itself sits thin enough to be felt by anyone with the sense to notice. The old imperial capital, built around the Dark Throne, is now among the very worst of these places, avoided even by those otherwise willing to brave the wilder parts of Arcanor — an empty seat radiating the exact wrongness it was built to contain. Kaelor, remote and separated by the same seas the exile ruined, has never suffered this the way the mainland has; distance is the only thing that has kept its own land whole. Arcanor itself no longer has that protection, since it sits where the wound actually is.

Over time, the real history faded from public knowledge. Most people now believe House Kaelarion has always been the rightful ruling dynasty of Kaelor. Only the current heads of the two houses — Lord Vesper Caldarion and King Aldric Kaelarion — still know the truth.

### The First Eclipse – The Founding of House Caldarion
Long before the Caldarion Empire existed, catastrophe struck the world. At midday, a tear appeared in the sky. The sun vanished, plunging the world into darkness as the Veil split open. From the breach emerged the **Primordial Darkness** — an ancient and terrifying force from beyond the Veil and the Eternal Current.

As kings, armies, and people fled in terror, one man remained. Rather than destroying the darkness, he **dominated it** and forced it back beyond the Veil. The tear closed. The world was saved.

After the victory, something extraordinary happened. Those who witnessed the event — commoners, lords, nobles, and kings alike — instinctively knelt before the man who had confronted the darkness. He never sought a crown. The people chose him, not because he conquered, but because he saved them.

The lord of what would become House Kaelarion was the first to kneel. It cost the family nothing to remember for most of the empire's history — one detail among thousands in the founding of a dynasty that would rule for centuries. It would come to matter enormously, much later, when almost nothing else from that founding day still remained.

This moment marked the founding of **House Caldarion**.

#### The Loyalist Phrase
In that same moment — as the witnesses knelt beneath the darkened sky — the first vassals and those who would become the line of true Loyalists spoke the words that would endure for centuries:

**“Hail the Monarch of the Dark.”**

The phrase was not created by House Caldarion. It was spoken *to* them by those who chose to follow. It became the formal loyalty hail of the old vassals and later of the hidden Loyalist lines that preserved memory of the true imperial house.

On Kaelor, the saying is almost entirely forgotten. Among certain mainland remnants and traditionalist families who still search for the vanished Caldarion line, it survives as ritual language — often without full understanding of its original weight.

**Later dramatic use:**
When true Loyalists first encounter Lucian Caldarion, they kneel and speak the ancient hail:

> “Hail the Monarch of the Dark.”

Lucian does not understand why they would say this to him. The moment should feel sudden, ceremonial, and unsettling. No full explanation is given at that time.

When the Veil closed, a **Black Star** emerged from the breach. It did not fall from the heavens — it came from the other side of the Veil itself. The material was unique: impossible to reproduce, unlike any material in the physical world — a fragment of the Eternal Current made physical.

Most of the Black Star was left where it landed. From it was carved **The Dark Throne** — the seat of emperors. The capital city of Arcanor was eventually built around it.

The Dark Throne served two purposes:
- **Public purpose**: The seat of Imperial authority.
- **Secret purpose**: A seal for the Primordial Darkness.

As long as a Lord of House Caldarion sat upon the throne, the seal remained stable. The world believed the Emperors ruled from the throne. The truth: they guarded it.

**The true mechanism (not known to any living Caldarion, including Vesper):** The Dark Throne draws Veil energy from whoever sits upon it. Drawing on that energy to stabilize the Veil itself is not unique to the bloodline — in principle, anyone with sufficient Veil energy and control could sit the throne and hold that half of the seal steady. Stabilizing the **Primordial Darkness** specifically, however, is unique to Caldarion blood alone; no amount of raw Veil strength in a non-Caldarion substitutes for it. At the time of the exile, the family believed — reasonably, given what they understood of the throne's workings — that a sufficiently capable successor dynasty could maintain the seal in their absence, and left believing the true danger was only ever going unheld, not going unheld *by them specifically*. No successor ever took the throne at all, leaving it empty rather than testing that belief — but even if one had, the seal on the Primordial Darkness would have failed regardless, since it was never a matter of strength alone. This distinction has been lost for centuries, unrecorded even in the family's own guarded histories, and no one now living — not Vesper, not any scholar, not even the Sisters of the Current — knows that the growing wrongness in the world traces back to an absence that only Caldarion blood, not merely a throne being occupied, could ever have prevented.

A fragment of the Black Star was forged into a sword. Its name: **Eclipse** — named after the day the world went dark.

**Characteristics**:
- Deep black blade made from the unique Black Star metal.
- No crossguard.
- Designed as a ruler’s blade rather than a duelist’s blade.

Eclipse became the symbol of Imperial authority. It was rarely drawn. Whenever it appeared, empires trembled, wars ended, and history changed.

Eclipse and the Dark Throne are made from the same Black Star. Together they form the heart of the Imperial authority.

A third fragment of the Black Star was set into a ring — the **Caldarion signet**, bearing the Black Sun sigil on its face. Where Eclipse is a weapon and the Dark Throne a seat, the ring is a tool of state: any wax or ink impression made with it carries a faint, unmistakable lingering trace of the Primordial Darkness, present in every genuine stamping regardless of what the sealed document concerns. That trace cannot be counterfeited by any means short of the ring itself, which is precisely its purpose — it is how an imperial decree, however old or however far the empire has fallen, can still be proven genuine rather than claimed. Unlike Eclipse and the Dark Throne, the ring left Arcanor with the family at the exile; it is the one piece of the Black Star that has never sat unguarded.

**Where Eclipse actually is — author-level fact, and the family does not know it.** The Dark Throne could not be moved and was left where it has always stood. **Eclipse was not abandoned in the throne room. It was secured inside the Imperial Vault** in the last days before the exile, and it is still there. What did not survive is the record of that decision — the departure was forced, hurried and politically hostile, and by the time the family had settled on Kaelor nobody could say with certainty what had been put behind that door. This is precisely why the house's own records "no longer specify" the Vault's contents (see `locations.md`): not because the knowledge was destroyed, but because it was never properly written down.

**Consequence:** the sword that was the symbol of imperial authority sits intact behind a lock that only the Caldarion signet opens, on a continent almost no one can reach — and the family holding the key has no idea the two things belong together. Whoever carries the ring back to Arcanor will find Eclipse waiting, whether or not that is what they went for.

The ring is more than a seal. It is the physical mark of headship, passed father to son together with the family's deepest secrets at the moment a new Lord Caldarion takes up the title — Morvath's survival, the truth of the ring itself, and everything else an heir has no need to know until the burden of the house is actually his to carry. It is also, and has always been, the only key to the **Imperial Vault** sealed beneath the old capital of Arcanor at the time of the exile — a lock no other means has ever opened, guarding something even the family's own records no longer specify, in a city now among the most dangerous ground left in the world. Vesper holds the ring now. He does not wear it openly — he keeps it on a chain beneath his clothing, close but unseen, in keeping with a house that has never once ruled from where anyone could watch it rule.

**No Crown Without Weight.** No Caldarion emperor, exiled lord, or head of house has ever worn a crown — not from any law against it, but from a saying old enough that no one alive can say which of its two readings the first Caldarions actually meant, and the ambiguity itself has outlived every attempt to settle it. Read one way, it is a warning: no one should wear a crown who has not first proven he can bear the weight of what wearing it costs, and the Caldarions, guardians of a seal the rest of the world doesn't know exists, pay that price constantly without ever needing a crown to announce it. Read the other way, it is closer to a boast turned humility: a Caldarion who already carries the true weight has no need of a crown at all — the symbol would only be redundant on someone who has already paid for the substance. Scholars who study the empire's founding age — Emeric Myrath among the very few outside House Caldarion to have looked closely — have never been able to determine which reading came first, or whether the founders meant both at once. What history agrees on is simpler: every Caldarion who has ever held real authority has held it by the plain fact of bearing it, crowned or not, and something in how they carry themselves has always made the distinction feel academic to whoever stood in front of them.

### The Bond Between Aldorion and Ravorn
King Aldorion Kaelarion (Aldric’s father) shared a deep, brother-like friendship with Lord Ravorn Caldarion. Their personal bond significantly reinforced the long-standing alliance between House Kaelarion and House Caldarion, helping secure the Kaelarion victory in the Last Continental War.

Lucian’s grandfather (the previous head of House Caldarion) was the last rider of the hidden dragon **Morvath**. He died in the Last Continental War, and it was publicly claimed that Morvath died with him. In reality, Morvath survived and has been kept hidden by House Caldarion ever since.

### The Last Continental War
A catastrophic war in which nearly every major and minor house on the Continent of Kaelor fought for power and resources. The victory of House Kaelarion, secured through the crucial support of House Caldarion, established the current royal dynasty and the present balance of power in the Kingdom of Kaelor.

**The Cause:** The Kaelarion king before Aldorion died without an heir whose claim was unquestioned, and Aldorion inherited the position young — visionary, but militarily unproven. It was exactly the kind of opening a rival house exploits, and no house had older reason to take it than Dravenhall. Four centuries before the war, Kaelor had been theirs alone to rule — lost not to conquest but to a single distant imperial decree they'd never had the power to refuse. That grievance had outlived every generation since, needing only a crack in Kaelarion's authority to become ambition again. House Dravenhall, the most militaristic of the Great Houses, led a coalition to break Aldorion's claim and redraw Kaelor's balance of power outright — this time, in their own telling, correcting an old wrong rather than committing a new one — joined by House Kestrion, whose forges supplied the rebellion's arms and siege engines. House Thalorin and House Vaelmar backed Kaelarion, though Vaelmar's early support was uncertain enough that Aldric would feel the need to secure it permanently decades later, through his second marriage to Lady Maris. House Myrath held to strict neutrality, as did House Sylvaron, which armed and traded with whichever side could pay and profited quietly from both.

**Why Caldarion Intervened:** Publicly, the reason has always been simple and warm: the deep, brother-like friendship between Ravorn Caldarion and Aldorion Kaelarion, one Great House standing by a beloved ally. That is true, and it is not the whole truth. House Kaelarion was the first house to kneel on the day of the First Eclipse — and after the exile and the closing of the seas behind it, it is now the only vassal House Caldarion has left in the world. For Ravorn, watching that last vassal face destruction was never merely a strategic problem to be weighed. It was the one failure his house could not survive with its identity intact — an empire, even a hidden, exiled one, that could not protect its own last subject was no empire at all, whether or not anyone else would ever know the difference. Ravorn committed House Caldarion's full hidden strength to the war for that reason above all others, and it was that strength, not sentiment alone, that ultimately decided it.

House Caldarion's contribution came at a severe cost. Ravorn died near the war's end, and with him a substantial number of the house's other direct and extended family — cousins, secondary branches, several who would ordinarily have stood between Vesper and the headship. By the war's end the direct line had been reduced to three living members: Vesper (twenty years old, suddenly head of house), his brother Dain, and their mother Orlena. The scale of that loss, more than the war's outcome itself, is what shaped the house's next decade.

**Why Dravenhall survived defeat intact:** Losing the war did not mean losing everything. Dravenhall's dragon, Korrath, was already large and formidable in the prime of his strength by the war's final years, and came through the fighting essentially undiminished, even as Caldarion and other houses lost dragons and riders they could not replace. A defeated house with a dragon like that at its back is not a house that can simply be crushed or annexed; finishing Dravenhall off would have meant fighting through Korrath directly, at a cost neither a war-exhausted Kaelarion nor a nearly extinguished House Caldarion could afford to pay. The peace that followed reflected that arithmetic: Dravenhall was humbled politically, stripped of any claim to the throne, and left to nurse a grievance it was never given the chance to avenge — but it kept its lands, its house, and its dragon, which is exactly why it is still capable of causing trouble a generation later.

**Why Morvath stays hidden:** He survived the war badly wounded and without a rider — a serious liability the house could not risk exposing while reduced to three people and surrounded by rivals like Dravenhall who resented Caldarion's strength even before the war's losses. What remains of the house recovered him quietly and has kept him that way ever since, preserving an undisclosed reserve of strength no one else knows exists.

This is not a secret kept from the crown itself, and it is not, strictly, illegal. Once it became clear Morvath had survived, Vesper — freshly head of house at twenty — told King Aldric personally, satisfying the law that all dragons and eggs must be reported to the crown. Nothing in that law requires the King to make such reports public, and Aldric, already several years into his reign, chose not to. The two of them agreed concealment served both houses better than disclosure — Caldarion too weakened to defend an unusual asset from scrutiny it hadn't earned, Kaelarion's own throne too dependent on Caldarion's strength to want that strength publicly diminished just as it had secured their crown. The secret has been theirs alone ever since.

The danger in it is not legal exposure — there is no law to expose. It is that no other Great House can know whether Morvath was ever properly reported at all, and a hidden, unridden dragon belonging to House Caldarion is exactly the kind of thing a rival like Dravenhall would make into a scandal regardless of the technical truth. Appearance, in this case, is more dangerous than the fact.

**The Succession Crisis (≈1030–1033):** With the line reduced to three and no margin left for further loss, Orlena pushed Vesper toward additional marriages — the conventional response for a great house facing exactly this kind of near-extinction, and hardly unusual by the standards of Kaelor's nobility more broadly. Multiple wives, and concubinage beneath a single principal wife, are ordinary practice among lords, kings, and emperors across the Great Houses when the security of a line is in question.

Vesper took the pressure and made his own decision from it. He was already betrothed to Elara Thalorin and wanted her, specifically, as his one true wife — and understood a practical consequence of marriage order: if he married anyone else first, Elara would become his third wife once their own wedding finally took place, whatever her betrothal predated. Concubinage was the only structure that let him rebuild the line's numbers without disturbing her precedence. Mira, and later Sable, were his own choice within that structure, not merely Orlena's compromise absorbed without feeling — he came to genuinely love them both, even as Elara remained, by his own will as much as by law, his one true wife. Lady Mira entered the household first, Lady Sable somewhat later. Their children — Lyra, and later the twins Riven and Thorne — were raised as full members of the house, though the hierarchy around Elara and her own children was never in question.

### Ancient Parallel: The First Kaelarion Veilseer
Long ago, a daughter of House Kaelarion manifested as a Veilseer. The Sisters of the Current arrived to claim her. House Kaelarion refused. House Caldarion intervened on behalf of their vassals. The conflict established a precedent of Caldarion protection over Kaelarion daughters who awakened Veilsight.

### Current Events – History Repeats
The first sustained contact between the isolated Continent of Kaelor and the mainland occurs because of Princess Seren Kaelarion. The Sisters of the Current sense the appearance of a new Veilseer and cross the Silent Belt (no-wind zone), the Storm Ring, and the Outer Deep to retrieve her.

The situation mirrors the ancient event:
- Then: A Kaelarion girl becomes a Veilseer → The order arrives → Kaelarion refuses → The Caldarions intervene.
- Now: Seren becomes a Veilseer → The Sisters arrive → The Kaelarions hesitate → Lucian and Vesper make Caldarion's position plain → **Aldric refuses.**

History repeats in the same shape as before: **the refusal is Kaelarion's, and Caldarion is what makes it possible.**

The Sisters formally request custody of Seren. The Kaelarions are hesitant — they respect the order but do not wish to lose their daughter, and no one wants to be the one to say so aloud.

**Lucian refuses first, and personally.** His position is simple and not diplomatic: Seren is his future wife, and nobody is taking her. **Vesper supports his son** — which settles what House Caldarion will do, in front of everyone in the room.

**Then Aldric refuses the Sisters.** The formal, binding refusal is the King's, not Caldarion's: it is his daughter and his crown, and the answer has to come from him. What makes him able to give it is knowing the strongest house on the continent has already declared where it stands. For the first time in centuries someone openly says no to the Sisters of the Current — and it is a reigning king who does it.

The Sisters are shocked, but not because they lost. **Nobody has ever openly refused them before** — not kings, not nobles, not rulers. Unable to enforce their demands, they withdraw. They intend to return.

**What Aldric alone understands.** He knows House Kaelarion are technically Caldarion's vassals (see "The True History of Kaelor"). So he is the only person in that hall who grasps why Vesper's declaration ends the argument rather than merely strengthening it — everyone else sees a powerful ally backing a king, while he sees something considerably older than his kingdom doing exactly what it did the last time this happened. He says nothing about it.

The Sisters did not travel alone. Their expedition included mainland nobles, scholars, merchants, and retainers. Many of them view Kaelor as a distant and insignificant land and consider Lucian a minor noble from the edge of the world. One of these mainland visitors publicly challenges or threatens Lucian and/or Seren. **Ancalion reacts instinctively to protect his bonded lord — but does not kill him.** He interposes himself and breathes flame across the ground between the man and Lucian, burning him without finishing him. The man is a minor member of an old mainland noble family, so the incident remains local for now — though he lives to carry the story, and the humiliation, back across the sea with him.

### Ripple in Arcanor
Some families in Arcanor still maintain old traditions. They possess little knowledge of the past. They only know: The true rulers of Arcanor were called **Caldarion**. Their symbol was the **Black Sun**. One day they vanished. Everything else has been lost.

When the dead man’s family receives news of the incident, the report includes a name: **Lucian Caldarion**. That single word changes everything. The family has spent generations searching for any clue. They possess no proof, yet the name alone is enough. They quietly organize a small expedition — not an army or a royal mission — with the goal of determining why someone in Kaelor bears the name Caldarion.

The loyalists do not discover the truth through books. They discover it through a **symbol**. When they reach Blackspire Keep they notice an ancient relic (an abandoned gate, an old wall, or a forgotten courtyard). Carved into it is the **Black Sun** — the symbol they have searched for over centuries.

## Prophecies & Legends

**There is exactly one prophecy in this setting, and it is not about Lucian.** The Prophecy — *"When the Dark Throne is forsaken, the world shall remember what it was made to forget"* — is documented in full above ("The True History of Kaelor → The Prophecy"). It concerns the throne and the seal, it was misread into a justification for the Caldarion exile, and it has nothing to do with a Dark Prince.

**There is no prophecy about a Dark Prince. This is deliberate and settled.** Nothing foretold Lucian, nothing was written about him before he existed, and no one is waiting for him. Do not invent one, and do not let any faction turn out to have been expecting him.

**"The Dark Prince" is a name the world gives him, not a destiny he was assigned.** It arises from what he actually does and from what people make of him — his house's reputation, the Black Sun, the Shadow/Void affinity, a black dragon with black flame, and in due course the things he chooses. He becomes it. He was not promised.

**Why this is the stronger version:** a prophesied heir is owed his significance; Lucian earns his, and every dark turn stays a choice rather than a fulfilment. It also keeps the **Sisters of the Current** honest when they arrive — their gift is perceiving *possible* futures, not fixed ones, so anything they see about Lucian is a branch and not a verdict. Nobody, including them, gets to tell him what he is.

## Faith and Oaths in Kaelor

**Kaelor has no organized religion, and that is deliberate rather than an omission.** There are no gods, temples, priesthoods, holy books or clergy anywhere on the continent. Do not introduce any.

**The reason is built into the setting: the Veil is real and can be studied.** House Myrath treats it as a scholarly discipline with archives, research and trained practitioners. Dragons fly overhead. Veilseers exist and are identified. Domains can be demonstrated in a closed room. Where the supernatural is empirically accessible and openly investigated, doctrine about the unseen has little room to form — Kaelor got **scholarship where another world would have got a church.**

**What does religion's structural work instead:**

- **House loyalty, which functions as faith in all but name.** Most visible in the North, where people believe House Caldarion keeps them safe "the way they believe the sun comes up" (Ch.67) — enough that most of Firsthold will bar their doors and wait to be proven right rather than flee. That is devotion, and it behaves like devotion.
- **Ancestral and lineage reverence.** House Caldarion's identity is its line: **Mercy**, the signet, the Black Sun, *No Crown Without Weight*. Reverence is directed at forebears and the continuity of the house, not upward at anything divine.
- **The Veil itself as the object of awe** — respected, feared, and understood as dangerous, but studied rather than worshipped.

**Oaths — what people swear on.** With no gods to invoke, binding words rest on **blood, name and house**: one's own life, one's line, and the honour of the house one belongs to. This is why **Blood Oaths** (see `magic-and-power-system.md`) are a real magical category rather than a metaphor — in a world without divine witness, the binding has to be literal. Marriages, fealty and sworn service all work the same way: the guarantee is lineage and reputation, and the penalty for breaking one is borne by the family as much as the man.

**Arcanor is different — see below.**

### Religion on Arcanor (reserved, not yet developed)

**Arcanor has a religion. Kaelor does not.** This contrast is intentional and should be preserved until Lucian eventually reaches Arcanor (see `notes-and-ideas.md`, the reserved chapter title **"Return"**).

Two hard constraints for whenever it gets built:

1. **It predates the Caldarions and is not about them.** It is older than the empire, older than the Dark Throne's occupancy by that family, and it is emphatically **not a Caldarion cult**. The Caldarions were emperors who held something the faith already had opinions about; they are at most a chapter in it, and possibly not a welcome one.
2. **It is a separate thread from the Black Sun remembrance.** The Arcanor families in "Ripple in Arcanor" keeping old traditions around a vanished house and its symbol are preserving a **dynastic memory**, not practising a religion. Whether the faith ever absorbed, opposed, or simply ignored the Black Sun is **an open question, deliberately undecided.**

**Natural anchor if one is wanted:** the oldest thing in the setting is the **Primordial Darkness and the seal that holds it**, and a faith predating the empire would plausibly have formed around exactly that — with doctrine about the current age, since the seal has been failing and the world demonstrably getting worse ever since the throne was abandoned. Flagged as a direction only. Nothing here is settled.
